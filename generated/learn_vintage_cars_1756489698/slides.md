## Vintage Cars
- Defined as vehicles manufactured between 1919 and 1930, characterized by features like external headlamps and running boards.
- Key identifying features include a hand crank for starting (pre-1925), a tall, narrow radiator grille, and artillery-style wooden wheels.
- Regular maintenance requires using non-detergent SAE 30-40 weight oil and checking mechanical brakes and ignition points every 500 miles.
- A 1929 Ford Model A in good condition is valued at approximately $15,000–$25,000, varying significantly with originality and provenance.
- Common upgrades for improved drivability include electronic ignition conversions and modern halogen headlight bulbs for night driving.

## Core Ideas
- **Hand-crank starting procedure**: Engage choke, set spark lever to retard, turn crank in short upward motions to avoid kickback injuries.
- **6-volt electrical system maintenance**: Requires specific 6V bulbs (e.g., #63 for taillights) and periodic generator brush replacement every ~15,000 miles.
- **Non-synchromesh gearbox operation**: Master double-declutching; rev-match between gears to prevent grinding, especially on 3-speed transmissions.
- **Brass radiator care**: Use distilled water with anti-corrosion tablets; never modern ethylene glycol coolant to prevent solder deterioration.
- **Timing adjustment with advance lever**: Manually adjust ignition timing via steering column lever; full advance for open road, retard for idling.

## How To Learn
- **Join a marque-specific club** like the Jaguar E-Type Club or Porsche 356 Registry for access to technical libraries, workshops, and expert mentorship.
- **Master factory workshop manuals** for your specific chassis number; e.g., a 1965 Mustang's manual details 289ci V8 valve lash adjustments to 0.017–0.019 inches.
- **Hands-on practice** is critical; start with simple tasks like setting points gap to 0.025 inches or adjusting twin SU carburetors for synchronized fuel flow.
- **Attend specialized schools** such as the McPherson College automotive restoration program for structured, hands-on curriculum.
- **Utilize online archives** like the Vintage Chevrolet Club of America's forum for troubleshooting specific issues, e.g., correcting a 1957 Bel Air's generator output.

## Examples
- 1930s Duesenberg Model J: Supercharged 420 cu in straight-eight engine producing 320 hp, with fewer than 500 built.
- 1957 Chevrolet Bel Air: Featured a 283 cu in V8 with optional fuel injection, generating 283 hp (1 hp per cu in).
- 1964.5 Ford Mustang: Launched with a 170 cu in inline-six or 260/289 cu in V8 options; sold over 400,000 units in its first year.
- 1963 Jaguar E-Type Series 1: 3.8L inline-six engine, 265 hp, and a top speed of 150 mph, famously praised for its design.
- 1955 Mercedes-Benz 300SL 'Gullwing': First production car with fuel injection and iconic upward-opening doors; 0–60 mph in ~8 seconds.

## Summary
- Pre-1960s vehicles are often classified as "veteran" or "vintage," while post-1925 classics are termed "post-vintage thoroughbred."
- A key preservation tip is to use ethanol-free fuel or a stabilizer to prevent corrosion in the carburetor and fuel lines.
- Regular maintenance should include checking the 6-volt electrical system (common in pre-1956 models) for voltage drop.
- When sourcing parts, reference the car's chassis number and engine displacement (e.g., a 1957 Chevrolet Bel Air with a 283 cu in V8).
- A proper concours restoration can require 2,000–3,000 hours and increase a car's value by 40–60%.
