## Eiffel Tower
- Constructed for the 1889 Exposition Universelle, standing at 330 meters (1,083 ft) including antennas
- Composed of 18,038 iron parts held together by 2.5 million rivets, weighing approximately 10,100 tons
- Painted in three shades of brown (darkest at the base, lightest at the top) and requires 60 tons of paint every 7 years
- Sways up to 9 cm (3.5 in) in the wind and expands up to 15 cm (6 in) in heat due to thermal expansion
- Hosts over 7 million visitors annually, making it the world's most-visited paid monument
- Features a secret apartment at the top built for Gustave Eiffel, now accessible to the public via guided tours

## Core Ideas
- Designed by Gustave Eiffel for the 1889 World's Fair, initially a temporary exhibit licensed for 20 years.
- Constructed from 18,038 individual iron parts joined by 2.5 million rivets over a period of 2 years, 2 months, and 5 days.
- Stands 330 meters (1,083 ft) tall, was the world's tallest man-made structure until the completion of the Chrysler Building in 1930.
- Sways up to 9 cm (3.5 in) in the wind and can shrink up to 15 cm (6 in) in cold weather due to thermal contraction of the iron.
- Requires repainting every 7 years, a process that uses approximately 60 tons of paint and takes 18 months to complete.
- Originally criticized by many Parisian artists and intellectuals as an eyesore, but is now the most-visited paid monument in the world.

- **Study structural engineering principles**, focusing on lattice design and wind resistance calculations, as the tower withstands winds up to 130 km/h.
- **Analyze historical construction timelines**, noting the 2 years, 2 months, and 5 days it took to build, and the specific phased assembly of 18,038 iron parts.
- **Memorize key numerical data**: height (300m to tip, 330m with antenna), weight (10,100 tons), and number of rivets (2.5 million) for technical accuracy.
- **Visit and observe the tower in person or via virtual tours**, paying attention to the curvature of the pillars designed for wind shear resistance.
- **Practice explaining its cultural significance**, including its role in 1889 Exposition Universelle and its initial criticism from Parisian artists.

## Examples
- Gustave Eiffel's company built the tower in 2 years, 2 months, and 5 days (1887–1889) with 300 on-site workers.
- The structure uses 18,038 iron pieces joined by 2.5 million rivets, weighing 7,300 tons (10,100 tons with non-metal components).
- It stands 330 meters tall (including antennas) and sways up to 9 cm in wind due to its flexible iron lattice design.
- Originally intended to be dismantled after 20 years, it was saved by its value for radio transmission experiments.
- The tower is repainted every 7 years using 60 tons of paint applied in three shades (darkest at base, lightest at top).
- It receives nearly 7 million visitors annually, making it the world's most-visited paid monument.

## Summary
- Constructed from 1887–1889 for the 1889 World's Fair, using 18,038 iron parts and 2.5 million rivets.
- Stands 330 meters (1,083 ft) tall, weighs approximately 10,100 tonnes, and sways up to 9 cm in wind.
- Requires repainting every 7 years, using 60 tonnes of paint applied in three shades (darkest at base).
- Hosts over 7 million annual visitors, with tickets priced from €10.70 (stairs to second floor) to €26.80 (elevator to top).
- Serves as a critical broadcast tower, transmitting radio and television signals across the Île-de-France region.
