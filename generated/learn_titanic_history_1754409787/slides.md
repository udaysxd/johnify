## Introduction to titanic history
- **Titanic history** explores the **construction, voyage, and sinking** of the RMS Titanic, the infamous "unsinkable" ship that met tragedy in 1912.
- It remains **relevant** as a lesson in **engineering, human error, and class disparities**, sparking safety reforms and cultural fascination.
- **Interesting fact**: Only **706 of the 2,224 passengers and crew** survived, with lifeboats launched at **half capacity** due to chaos and miscommunication.

## Slide 2
- **Construction and Design**: The Titanic was a **luxury ocean liner** built by the White Star Line, designed to be the largest and most advanced ship of its time. Its **"unsinkable" reputation** made its sinking even more shocking, highlighting overconfidence in engineering.
- **Maiden Voyage Disaster**: The Titanic struck an **iceberg** on April 14, 1912, and sank within hours, resulting in over 1,500 deaths. This tragedy exposed major flaws in **safety regulations**, like insufficient lifeboats, leading to stricter maritime laws.
- **Cultural Impact**: The Titanic's story became a **symbol of human ambition and tragedy**, inspiring books, films, and memorials. It remains a **cautionary tale** about hubris and the limits of technology.

## How titanic history Works
- **Construction and Design**: The Titanic was built as the **largest and most luxurious ship** of its time, featuring advanced safety measures like watertight compartments. Its grandeur symbolized **human engineering pride** but also overconfidence in its "unsinkable" claim.
- **Maiden Voyage and Collision**: On April 10, 1912, the Titanic set sail from Southampton, hitting an **iceberg on April 14** due to high speed and insufficient warnings. This disaster revealed critical flaws in **maritime safety protocols** and emergency preparedness.
- **Aftermath and Legacy**: The sinking led to **1,500+ deaths** and sparked global reforms, including stricter lifeboat requirements and the creation of the **International Ice Patrol**. The tragedy remains a **cautionary tale** about hubris and the importance of safety over luxury.
