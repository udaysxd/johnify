## Taj Mahal
- Constructed between 1632 and 1653 by Mughal Emperor Shah Jahan as a mausoleum for his wife Mumtaz Mahal
- Built from white marble sourced from Makrana, Rajasthan, using an estimated 20,000 laborers and 1,000 elephants
- Architectural style combines Persian, Islamic, and Indian influences, standing 73 meters (240 feet) tall at its central dome
- Complex includes a main gateway, garden, mosque, guest house, and reflecting pools, covering 17 hectares (42 acres)
- Inscribed as a UNESCO World Heritage Site in 1983, attracting 7–8 million visitors annually
- Conservation efforts focus on reducing environmental damage from air pollution and foot traffic erosion

## Core Ideas
- Built between 1632 and 1653 by Mughal Emperor Shah Jahan as a mausoleum for his wife Mumtaz Mahal.
- Constructed primarily from white marble, sourced from Makrana, Rajasthan, using an estimated 20,000 artisans.
- The main structure stands 73 meters (240 feet) tall and is flanked by four 40-meter (131-foot) minarets.
- The complex spans approximately 17 hectares (42 acres) and includes a mosque, a guest house, and formal gardens.
- Inlaid with intricate designs using 28 types of precious and semi-precious stones in a technique known as *pietra dura*.
- Designated a UNESCO World Heritage Site in 1983 and attracts 7–8 million visitors annually.

## How To Learn
- **Visit during sunrise or sunset** to see the marble change color with the light, avoiding midday crowds and heat.
- **Study the inlay work up close** by examining the pietra dura floral patterns on the walls, noting the use of 43 different gemstone types.
- **Listen to a guided audio tour** for 90 minutes to understand the symbolism, like the four minarets tilting outward to protect the main tomb.
- **Read the calligraphy** on the main gateway, which increases in size with height to maintain visual consistency from the ground.
- **Compare architectural styles** by observing the blend of Persian, Islamic, and Indian designs in the dome, arches, and gardens.

## Examples
- **Construction Period:** Built between 1632 and 1653, employing an estimated 20,000 artisans and laborers.
- **Primary Materials:** Constructed using translucent white marble from Makrana, Rajasthan, inlaid with 28 types of precious and semi-precious stones.
- **Architectural Symmetry:** The complex is perfectly symmetrical along its central axis, except for the cenotaph of Shah Jahan, which was added later.
- **Gardens (Charbagh):** The Mughal garden is divided into four quadrants by two marble canals, representing the four rivers of Paradise.
- **Calligraphic Inscriptions:** Verses from the Quran are inscribed in black marble on the main gateway and the mausoleum, with the letter size subtly increasing with height to maintain visual consistency.

## Summary
- Constructed between 1632 and 1653 by Mughal Emperor Shah Jahan as a mausoleum for his wife Mumtaz Mahal.
- Located in Agra, India, on the southern bank of the Yamuna River, covering an area of 42 acres (17 hectares).
- Built primarily from white marble, with materials sourced from across India and Asia, including jade from China and turquoise from Tibet.
- Architectural style is a blend of Persian, Islamic, and Indian influences, featuring a large central dome (73 meters high) and four minarets.
- Designated a UNESCO World Heritage Site in 1983 and attracts an estimated 7–8 million visitors annually.
