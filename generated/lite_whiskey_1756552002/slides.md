## Whiskey
*   **Defined by Grain & Barrel:** Legally, whiskey must be distilled from fermented grain mash (e.g., corn, barley, rye, wheat) and aged in wooden containers, primarily new charred oak barrels.
*   **Major Types by Region:** Scotch (Scotland, smoky from peat), Bourbon (USA, ≥51% corn, new charred oak), Irish (Ireland, triple-distilled, smoother), and Rye (USA/Canada, ≥51% rye, spicier).
*   **Proof & ABV:** "Proof" is double the Alcohol By Volume (ABV). An 80 proof whiskey is 40% alcohol. Cask strength bottlings can exceed 110 proof (55% ABV).
*   **Tasting Steps:** Use a tulip-shaped glass. First nose it, then take a small sip to "chew," letting it coat your palate to detect sweet, spicy, oaky, or fruity notes.
*   **Adding Water:** A few drops of water can open up the aromas and soften the alcohol burn, making complex flavors more accessible without diluting the experience.

## Core Ideas
- **Whiskey is distilled from fermented grain mash**—typically barley, corn, rye, or wheat—and must be aged in wooden casks.
- **Legally, Scotch must be aged for at least 3 years** in Scotland, while Bourbon requires a mash of at least 51% corn and new charred oak barrels.
- **The 'angel's share' is about 2% of volume lost to evaporation** each year during aging, concentrating the flavor.
- **Serve in a tulip-shaped glass** to concentrate aromas; adding a few drops of water can open up more complex flavors.
- **Major types include Scotch, Bourbon, Irish, and Rye**, each defined by grain, region, and production rules.

## How To Learn
- **Taste 3–5 whiskeys side-by-side** to compare styles (e.g., Scotch vs. Bourbon)
- **Use a Glencairn glass** to better detect aromas like vanilla, smoke, or citrus
- **Add a few drops of water** to open up flavors and reduce alcohol burn
- **Start with lower proof (80-90)** before exploring higher proof or cask strength bottles
- **Take notes on flavor profiles** using common descriptors: sweet, spicy, smoky, fruity

## Examples
- **Single Malt Scotch**: Must be from one distillery, 100% malted barley, aged at least 3 years in oak casks (e.g., Glenfiddich 12 Year Old).
- **Bourbon**: American whiskey made from at least 51% corn, aged in new charred oak barrels (e.g., Maker's Mark).
- **Irish Whiskey**: Typically triple-distilled for smoothness, made from a mix of malted and unmalted barley (e.g., Jameson).
- **Japanese Whisky**: Often inspired by Scotch, known for precise blending and delicate flavors (e.g., Hibiki Harmony).

## Summary
- Whiskey is distilled from fermented grain mash and aged in wooden casks, legally requiring at least 3 years of aging in many regions.
- Main types include Scotch (malted barley), Bourbon (≥51% corn, new charred oak), and Irish (triple-distilled, smoother).
- Proof indicates alcohol content; 80 proof equals 40% ABV, with cask strength often exceeding 55%.
- Tasting involves nosing for aromas like vanilla or smoke, then sipping to identify sweet, spicy, or woody notes.
- Store bottles upright, away from light and heat, to preserve flavor and prevent cork deterioration.
