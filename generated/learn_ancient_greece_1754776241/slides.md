## Introduction to ancient greece
- **Ancient Greece** was a civilization that thrived from around 800 BCE to 146 BCE, known for its **advancements in democracy, philosophy, and art**. It laid the foundation for Western culture.
- Its influence remains **relevant today** in fields like government (democracy), science (mathematics), and literature (epic poems like the *Odyssey*).
- **Fun fact:** The ancient Greeks held the **first Olympic Games** in 776 BCE, a tradition revived in 1896 as the modern Olympics.

## Slide 2
- **City-States (Polis)** - Ancient Greece was made up of independent **city-states** like Athens and Sparta, each with its own government and laws. This matters because it led to diverse political systems and cultural achievements.
- **Democracy** - Athens developed the world’s first **democracy**, where male citizens could vote on laws and policies. This matters because it laid the foundation for modern democratic governments.
- **Philosophy and Science** - Greek thinkers like **Socrates, Plato, and Aristotle** pioneered philosophy, logic, and scientific inquiry. This matters because their ideas still influence Western thought and education today.

## Examples of ancient greece
- **The Parthenon**: A temple dedicated to **Athena**, built in the 5th century BCE on the **Acropolis of Athens**. Its **architectural brilliance** and **sculptural decorations** symbolize the peak of **Ancient Greek art and democracy**.
- **The Olympic Games**: Originating in **776 BCE** in Olympia, these games were a **pan-Hellenic festival** honoring Zeus. They fostered **unity among city-states** and laid the foundation for the **modern Olympic tradition**.
- **Theater of Epidaurus**: A **well-preserved ancient theater** built in the 4th century BCE, renowned for its **acoustics and design**. It highlights the Greeks' **cultural emphasis on drama** and public performance.
