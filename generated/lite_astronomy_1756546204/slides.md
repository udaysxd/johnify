## Astronomy
- The observable universe spans approximately 93 billion light-years in diameter and contains an estimated 2 trillion galaxies.
- Our solar system's eight planets orbit the Sun counterclockwise in elliptical paths, with Mercury completing an orbit in 88 days and Neptune in 165 years.
- Stars undergo nuclear fusion, converting hydrogen to helium; our Sun (a G-type main-sequence star) has a surface temperature of 5,500°C.
- Telescopes collect light using mirrors or lenses; the Hubble Space Telescope's 2.4-meter mirror has observed objects 13.4 billion light-years away.
- The Moon orbits Earth every 27.3 days, causing phases due to the changing angle of sunlight; full moons occur when Earth lies between the Sun and Moon.
- Exoplanets are detected via the transit method (measuring stellar brightness dips) or radial velocity (tracking stellar wobbles), revealing over 5,500 confirmed worlds.

## Core Ideas
- The universe is approximately 13.8 billion years old, starting from the Big Bang.
- Our solar system has 8 planets: 4 rocky inner planets and 4 gas/ice giants.
- Stars generate energy through nuclear fusion, converting hydrogen into helium.
- A galaxy is a massive gravitationally bound system; our Milky Way contains 100–400 billion stars.
- The observable universe is estimated to be 93 billion light-years in diameter.

## How To Learn
- **Observe the night sky weekly**; track moon phases and planet positions using free apps like Stellarium.
- **Master magnitude scales**: recognize that a difference of 5 magnitudes equals a 100x change in brightness.
- **Learn the 88 constellations**; start with the zodiac and major ones like Orion and Ursa Major.
- **Join a local astronomy club** for guided telescope viewing sessions and mentorship from experienced observers.
- **Practice sketching what you see** through a telescope; this improves detail recognition and observational patience.

## Examples
- **The Moon's diameter**: 3,474 km, about 1/4 the size of Earth.
- **Distance to the Sun**: 149.6 million km, known as 1 Astronomical Unit (AU).
- **Andromeda Galaxy**: 2.5 million light-years away, our closest major galaxy.
- **Betelgeuse**: A red supergiant star with a diameter ~1,000 times larger than the Sun.
- **The Hubble Deep Field**: Image revealing ~3,000 galaxies in a tiny patch of sky.

## Summary
- The observable universe spans approximately 93 billion light-years in diameter.
- Our solar system is located in the Orion Arm of the Milky Way galaxy, roughly 27,000 light-years from the galactic center.
- The eight planets orbit the Sun in elliptical paths, with Mercury completing an orbit in just 88 Earth days.
- Stars are classified by temperature and luminosity on the Hertzsprung-Russell diagram; our Sun is a G-type main-sequence star.
- A light-year, the distance light travels in one year, equals about 9.46 trillion kilometers.
