## Dinosaurs Extinction
- **Primary Cause:** A massive asteroid, approximately 10–15 km wide, struck Earth at Chicxulub, Mexico, 66 million years ago.
- **Immediate Effects:** The impact released energy equivalent to 10 billion Hiroshima atomic bombs, triggering global wildfires, mega-tsunamis, and a seismic pulse.
- **Long-Term Climate Impact:** Soot and sulfate aerosols from the impact and fires blocked sunlight for up to a decade, causing a global winter with surface temperature drops of 15–25°C.
- **Ocean Acidification:** Sulfur dioxide in the atmosphere led to severe ocean acidification, collapsing marine food webs by dissolving the shells of plankton.
- **Extinction Pattern:** The event caused the extinction of all non-avian dinosaurs and approximately 75% of all plant and animal species on Earth.
- **Evidence:** The global K-Pg boundary layer contains iridium (rare on Earth but common in asteroids), shocked quartz, and soot deposits, confirming the impact hypothesis.

## Core Ideas
- **Impact Event**: A 10–15 km wide asteroid struck the Yucatán Peninsula, Mexico, creating the 180 km wide Chicxulub crater and releasing energy equivalent to billions of atomic bombs.
- **Immediate Aftermath**: The impact triggered global wildfires, megatsunamis over 100 meters high, and launched vast quantities of vaporized rock and sulfates into the atmosphere.
- **Climate Collapse**: Injected aerosols blocked sunlight for years, causing a rapid "impact winter" with global temperatures dropping by up to 25°C and photosynthesis to cease.
- **Ocean Acidification**: CO₂ released from vaporized carbonate rock dissolved in oceans, rapidly acidifying marine environments and dissolving the shells of plankton and mollusks.
- **Cascade Extinction**: 76% of all species, including all non-avian dinosaurs, went extinct due to the collapse of food webs; the event marks the end of the Cretaceous period 66 million years ago.

## How To Learn
- **Study fossil layers** to identify the K-Pg boundary, where iridium spikes and shocked quartz indicate an asteroid impact 66 million years ago.
- **Analyze global sediment samples** for evidence of massive wildfires and soot deposits, which suggest widespread environmental devastation following impact.
- **Compare extinction rates** across species; non-avian dinosaurs show 100% extinction, while mammals and birds survived due to smaller size and varied diets.
- **Model climate effects** using computer simulations to understand impact winter scenarios, including temperature drops of up to 20°C lasting decades.
- **Examine Deccan Traps volcanism** in India to assess its role, studying lava flows covering 500,000 km² and their potential contribution to longer-term climate change.

## Examples
- **Impact winter hypothesis**: A 10–15 km asteroid impact released energy equivalent to 100 trillion tons of TNT, ejecting 25 trillion metric tons of debris, blocking sunlight for years and collapsing photosynthesis-based food chains.
- **Deccan Traps volcanism**: Sustained eruptions in modern-day India released over 1.1 million cubic kilometers of lava and 10 trillion tons of CO₂ and SO₂, causing severe global warming followed by acid rain and ocean acidification over 30,000 years.
- **Iridium anomaly evidence**: A global clay layer shows iridium concentrations 30–160 times higher than background levels, strongly supporting extraterrestrial impact due to the element’s abundance in asteroids.
- **Fern spike phenomenon**: Pollen records reveal a sudden dominance of fern spores immediately post-impact, indicating rapid ecosystem collapse and pioneer species recolonization in devastated landscapes.
- **Selective marine extinction**: 90% of foraminifera species vanished due to ocean acidification, while organisms less dependent on calcification, like some mollusks, showed higher survival rates.

## Summary
- The Chicxulub impact event, a 10–15 km wide asteroid, struck modern-day Mexico 66 million years ago, releasing energy equivalent to 10 billion Hiroshima atomic bombs.
- The impact triggered a cascade of effects: global wildfires, megatsunamis, and a long-lasting "impact winter" caused by sunlight-blocking aerosols in the atmosphere.
- This led to the collapse of photosynthesis-based food webs, with an estimated 75% of all species, including all non-avian dinosaurs, going extinct.
- Evidence is found globally in a thin layer of sediment, the K-Pg boundary, which is enriched with iridium—an element rare on Earth but common in asteroids.
- The extinction created ecological niches that allowed mammals and eventually birds (theropod dinosaurs) to diversify and dominate terrestrial ecosystems.
