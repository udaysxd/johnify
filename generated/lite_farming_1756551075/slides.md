## Farming
- **Crop rotation cycles**: Rotate legumes (soybeans), grains (corn), and cover crops (rye) on a 3–4 year schedule to replenish soil nitrogen and reduce pest buildup by 40–60%.
- **Precision irrigation**: Use soil moisture sensors and drip systems to apply 0.5–0.7 inches of water per week directly to plant roots, cutting water usage by up to 50% compared to flood irrigation.
- **Integrated pest management (IPM)**: Scout fields weekly; introduce beneficial insects like ladybugs (1,500/acre) and apply targeted pesticides only when pest thresholds exceed 5 insects per plant.
- **Soil testing protocol**: Collect soil samples from 15–20 locations per acre every 3 years; maintain pH between 6.0–7.0 and phosphorus levels at 30–50 ppm for optimal nutrient availability.
- **Harvest timing indicators**: Harvest corn at 18–24% kernel moisture and soybeans when pods are tan and seeds rattle; delays can cause 5–10% yield loss from shattering or disease.

## Core Ideas
- Soil pH should be maintained between 6.0 and 7.0 for most row crops; test annually and amend with lime if too acidic.
- Rotate crops (e.g., corn → soybeans → wheat) to break pest cycles and improve soil nitrogen, reducing fertilizer needs by ~15%.
- Use integrated pest management: scout fields weekly and only apply pesticides when pest counts exceed 5–10 insects per plant.
- Ensure 1–1.5 inches of water per week during critical growth stages like pollination for optimal yield.
- Calibrate planting equipment to achieve a target seeding rate of 30,000–35,000 corn seeds per acre for maximum efficiency.

## How To Learn
- **Start with soil testing**: Analyze pH and nutrient levels annually; aim for 6.0–7.0 pH for most crops.
- **Master crop rotation**: Plan a 3–4 year cycle (e.g., corn → soybeans → wheat → cover crop) to prevent disease.
- **Calculate precise inputs**: Apply fertilizer based on soil test results, not exceeding 150 lbs/acre of nitrogen for corn.
- **Implement integrated pest management**: Scout fields weekly; treat only when pest counts exceed economic thresholds.
- **Use water efficiently**: Install drip irrigation to reduce usage by 30–50% compared to overhead systems.
- **Keep detailed records**: Log planting dates, yields, and weather to identify patterns and improve decisions.

## Examples
- **Crop rotation**: Plant corn one year, soybeans the next to reduce soil-borne diseases and improve nitrogen levels.
- **Precision irrigation**: Use soil moisture sensors to apply exactly 1.5 inches of water per week, reducing usage by 25%.
- **Integrated pest management**: Introduce 150 ladybugs per acre to control aphids instead of broad-spectrum pesticides.
- **Cover cropping**: Sow winter rye after harvest to prevent erosion and add 30 lbs of nitrogen per acre to the soil.
- **No-till farming**: Leave crop residues on fields to increase organic matter by 0.1% annually and conserve moisture.

## Summary
- Crop rotation cycles (e.g., corn-soybeans-wheat) reduce soil-borne diseases by up to 40% and improve nitrogen fixation.
- Precision agriculture uses GPS and soil sensors to apply water and fertilizer in variable rates, cutting input costs by ~15%.
- Integrated Pest Management combines biological controls (e.g., ladybugs for aphids) with targeted chemical use only when pest thresholds are exceeded.
- No-till farming preserves soil structure, increases water retention by 20%, and sequesters approximately 0.3 tons of carbon per acre annually.
- Drip irrigation systems deliver water directly to plant roots, achieving 90-95% efficiency compared to 60-70% for traditional overhead sprinklers.
