## Architecture
- **Load-bearing walls** must be at least 200mm thick for a two-story residential structure.
- **Egress windows** in bedrooms require a minimum opening area of 0.33m² and a sill height no greater than 1.1m from the floor.
- **Stair design** follows the 7-11 rule: a maximum riser height of 7 inches and a minimum tread depth of 11 inches.
- **Foundation depth** must extend at least 450mm below grade to prevent frost heave in temperate climates.
- **Roof pitch** for asphalt shingles should be a minimum of 4:12 (18.4 degrees) for proper water drainage.

## Core Ideas
- **Form follows function**: Design must prioritize purpose over aesthetics, as seen in Louis Sullivan’s skyscrapers.
- **Scale and proportion**: Use the golden ratio (1:1.618) to achieve visual harmony in elements like window spacing.
- **Material integrity**: Employ materials truthfully; e.g., steel for tensile strength, not disguised as stone.
- **Spatial sequencing**: Create experience through progression, like the deliberate narrowing of corridors in Gothic cathedrals.
- **Contextual integration**: Designs must respond to environment, climate, and existing structures.

## How To Learn
- **Study landmark buildings** like the Parthenon or Fallingwater to understand core design principles and historical context.
- **Practice sketching floor plans** daily, starting with a simple 100m² single-family home to develop spatial reasoning.
- **Build physical models** using basswood or chipboard at 1:50 scale to translate 2D drawings into 3D forms.
- **Master one software tool** thoroughly (e.g., AutoCAD for drafting or SketchUp for modeling) before learning others.
- **Visit construction sites** (with permission) to observe how architectural drawings are realized with real materials and techniques.

## Examples
- **Gothic cathedrals** (12th–16th c.): Characterized by pointed arches, flying buttresses, and ribbed vaults, e.g., Notre-Dame de Paris (construction began 1163).
- **Modernist skyscrapers**: Emphasize form following function with steel frames and curtain walls, e.g., Seagram Building, NYC (1958), 515 ft tall.
- **Traditional Japanese joinery**: Uses intricate wood connections without nails; e.g., mortise-and-tenon joints require precision within 1 mm tolerance.
- **Passive house standard**: Buildings must achieve an annual heating demand ≤ 15 kWh/m² and airtightness < 0.6 ACH at 50 Pa.

## Summary
- Classical orders (Doric, Ionic, Corinthian) defined by specific column and entablature proportions, e.g., Doric column height is 5–6 times its lower diameter.
- Gothic architecture introduced the pointed arch and flying buttress, allowing walls to reach over 30 meters with expansive stained-glass windows.
- Modernist principle "form follows function" prioritizes purpose over ornament, using materials like steel and glass for open-plan spaces.
- Sustainable design now mandates strategies like using 40% recycled materials and achieving net-zero energy consumption in new builds.
- BIM (Building Information Modeling) creates intelligent 3D models that integrate cost, scheduling, and sustainability data for entire project lifecycles.
