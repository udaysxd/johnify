## Hungarian: A Unique Language
- Agglutinative language: words built by adding suffixes.
- No grammatical gender: no 'he', 'she', 'it'.
- Vowel harmony: affects suffix endings.
- Part of the Uralic language family.
- Spoken primarily in Hungary.

<!-- NARRATION: Welcome to the fascinating world of Hungarian! It's a unique language known for its agglutinative nature, meaning words are built by adding many suffixes. You'll also find it doesn't have grammatical gender, and vowel harmony plays a crucial role in how words are formed. -->

## The Hungarian Alphabet
- Uses the Latin alphabet with 14 extra letters/digraphs.
- Includes unique sounds like 'cs', 'gy', 'ly', 'ny', 'sz', 'ty', 'zs'.
- Diphthongs and trigraphs create new sounds.
- Pronunciation is generally phonetic.
- Accents indicate long vowels (á, é, í, ó, ö, ő, ú, ü, ű).

<!-- NARRATION: The Hungarian alphabet is based on the Latin script but includes 14 extra letters and digraphs that represent distinct sounds. Learning these unique letter combinations is key to pronunciation, which is generally quite phonetic, making it easier to sound words out once you know the rules. -->

## Essential Hungarian Greetings
- Szia/Sziasztok: Hi/Hello (informal/plural).
- Jó napot: Good day (formal).
- Jó reggelt: Good morning.
- Jó estét: Good evening.
- Viszlát: Goodbye (informal).
- Viszontlátásra: Goodbye (formal).

## Basic Sentence Structure
- Subject-Verb-Object (SVO) is common, but flexible.
- Word order can change for emphasis.
- Verbs are conjugated for person, number, and tense.
- Possession is shown with suffixes.
- No articles like 'a' or 'an'.

<!-- NARRATION: Hungarian sentence structure is generally Subject-Verb-Object, but it's more flexible than you might expect. The order of words can be shifted to emphasize different parts of the sentence. Verbs are heavily conjugated, and possession is often indicated by suffixes rather than separate words. -->

## Key Hungarian Vocabulary
- Igen: Yes
- Nem: No
- Köszönöm: Thank you
- Kérem: Please
- Bocsánat: Excuse me/Sorry
- Egy: One, Kettő: Two, Három: Three
