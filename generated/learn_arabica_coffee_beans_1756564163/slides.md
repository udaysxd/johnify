## Arabica Coffee Beans
- **Optimal Growing Conditions**: Requires 15–24°C temperatures, 1500–2000mm annual rainfall, and high altitudes (800–2200m) for slow maturation and complex sugars.
- **Bean Characteristics**: Recognizable by elongated, oval shape with a curved crease; typically contains 0.8–1.4% caffeine by weight.
- **Harvesting Method**: Primarily hand-picked to select only ripe cherries, ensuring uniform ripeness and minimizing damage to trees.
- **Processing Variations**: Often wet-processed (washed) to enhance acidity and clarity, though natural and honey methods are used for fruitier profiles.
- **Roasting Profile**: Best at medium roasts (City to Full City) to preserve delicate floral, fruity, and sugary notes without overpowering bitterness.
- **Brewing Recommendation**: Use water at 90–96°C with a 1:16 coffee-to-water ratio for balanced extraction of nuanced flavors.

## Core Ideas
- Arabica beans require specific growing conditions: 15–24°C, 1500–2000mm annual rainfall, and high altitudes (900–2000m).
- Harvesting is typically done by selective hand-picking to ensure only ripe cherries are collected, often requiring multiple passes.
- Processing methods significantly impact flavor: washed (clean, acidic), natural (fruity, heavy body), or honey (balanced sweetness).
- Roast profiles vary by origin; light roasts highlight acidity and floral notes, while medium roasts develop balanced sweetness and body.
- Store beans in airtight containers away from light, heat, and moisture; consume within 3–4 weeks of roasting for peak flavor.

## How To Learn
- **Taste at least 10 different single-origin coffees** from countries like Ethiopia, Colombia, and Guatemala to calibrate your palate to regional flavor profiles.
- **Master the "coffee cupping" protocol**: use a specific 12g of grounds to 200ml of water at 200°F, steep for 4 minutes, break the crust, and slurp to aerate.
- **Keep a detailed tasting journal**, scoring aroma, acidity, body, and flavor on a 10-point scale for each sample to track your sensory development.
- **Join a certification program** like the SCA's Sensory Skills or Intermediate Roasting courses to get structured, professional feedback on your progress.

## Examples
- **Ethiopian Yirgacheffe**: Grown at 1,900–2,200 meters, known for bright acidity, floral notes, and a light to medium body.
- **Colombian Supremo**: Cultivated in the Andes at 1,200–1,800 meters, features a balanced profile with notes of caramel and nut, and medium acidity.
- **Brazilian Santos**: Typically grown at 800–1,300 meters, offers a low-acidity, smooth cup with chocolate and nutty undertones.
- **Guatemalan Antigua**: Grown in volcanic soil at 1,500–1,700 meters, characterized by a full body, spicy complexity, and smoky sweetness.

## Summary
- Arabica beans (Coffea arabica) comprise ~60% of global coffee production, prized for their smooth, complex flavor profile with notes of sugar, fruit, and berries.
- Optimal growing conditions require high altitudes (600–2200 meters), consistent temperatures (15–24°C), and significant annual rainfall (1500–2000mm).
- Processing methods significantly impact flavor: washed processing yields a cleaner, brighter cup, while natural processing enhances body and fruitiness.
- Key quality indicators include a strict defect allowance (max. 5 full defects per 300g sample for Grade 1) and a screen size typically between 15-18.
- Roasting is best done to medium (City to Full City) levels to preserve inherent acidity and nuanced sugar browning flavors, avoiding dark roasts that overpower delicate note
