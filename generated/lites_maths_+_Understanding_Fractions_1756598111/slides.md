## What are Fractions?
- A fraction represents a part of a whole.
- It has two main parts: numerator and denominator.
- The denominator shows how many equal parts the whole is divided into.
- The numerator shows how many of those parts we have.

<!-- NARRATION: Welcome to our journey into understanding fractions! Think of a fraction as a way to talk about parts of something whole, like sharing a pizza. It's a fundamental concept in math that helps us describe portions. -->

---

## Numerator & Denominator
- Numerator: The top number (e.g., 1 in 1/2).
- Denominator: The bottom number (e.g., 2 in 1/2).
- Denominator tells us the total equal parts.
- Numerator tells us the selected parts.
- Together, they describe a portion of a whole.

<!-- NARRATION: Every fraction has two key numbers: the numerator and the denominator. The denominator tells us how many equal pieces the whole is divided into, and the numerator tells us how many of those pieces we're considering. It's like having a clear label for each part. -->

## Visualizing Fractions
- Think of a pizza or a cake!
- A whole pizza is 1/1.
- Cutting it into 4 equal slices means the denominator is 4.
- If you eat 1 slice, you've eaten 1/4.
- 2 slices eaten is 2/4.

<!-- NARRATION: Visualizing fractions makes them much easier to grasp. Imagine a pizza cut into several equal slices; that's your denominator. If you take a few slices, that's your numerator, showing how much of the pizza you have. -->

---

## Types of Fractions
- Proper Fractions: Numerator is smaller than the denominator (e.g., 3/4).
- Improper Fractions: Numerator is greater than or equal to the denominator (e.g., 5/4).
- Mixed Numbers: A whole number and a proper fraction (e.g., 1 1/4).

<!-- NARRATION: We encounter different types of fractions in our daily lives. Proper fractions represent less than a whole, like half an apple. Improper fractions represent a whole or more, and mixed numbers combine a whole number with a fraction for convenience. -->

---

## Why Fractions Matter
- Used in cooking: recipes often use fractions.
- Sharing: dividing food or objects equally.
- Measuring: lengths, weights, and time.
- Understanding proportions and ratios.
- Foundation for more advanced math concepts.
