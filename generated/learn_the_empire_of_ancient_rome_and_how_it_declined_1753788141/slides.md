## Introduction to the empire of ancient rome and how it declined?
- The **Roman Empire** was one of history's most powerful civilizations, ruling vast territories for centuries before its **gradual decline** due to political corruption, economic crises, and invasions.
- Understanding Rome's fall is **relevant today** because it offers lessons about governance, military overextension, and societal resilience in the face of collapse.
- **Interesting fact:** At its peak, Rome controlled **2.5 million square miles** of land, but by 476 CE, the Western Empire had collapsed under Germanic invasions.

## Slide 2
- **Military Overextension** - The Roman Empire grew too large, making it difficult to defend its borders from invasions. This weakened the army and drained resources, contributing to decline.
- **Political Instability** - Frequent changes in leadership, corruption, and civil wars disrupted governance. This made the empire vulnerable to crises and external threats.
- **Economic Troubles** - Heavy taxation, inflation, and reliance on slave labor hurt trade and productivity. A failing economy weakened Rome’s ability to sustain its empire.

## How the empire of ancient rome and how it declined? Works
- **Political Instability** – Frequent changes in leadership due to assassinations, civil wars, and weak emperors weakened Rome's governance. This made it hard to maintain order or long-term policies.
- **Economic Troubles** – Heavy taxation, inflation, and reliance on slave labor strained the economy. The empire struggled to fund its military and public services, leading to decline.
- **External Invasions** – Barbarian tribes like the Goths and Vandals attacked Rome's borders as defenses weakened. These invasions eventually led to the fall of the Western Roman Empire in 476 CE.
