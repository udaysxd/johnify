## Coffee
- **Brewing ratio**: Use 60 grams of coffee per 1 liter of water (a 1:16.67 ratio) for optimal extraction.
- **Grind size matters**: Espresso requires a fine, powdery grind, while French press needs a coarse, breadcrumb-like consistency.
- **Water temperature**: Ideal brewing occurs between 195°F and 205°F (90°C–96°C); boiling water scorches the beans.
- **Two main species**: *Coffea arabica* (sweeter, complex) makes up 60-70% of global production; *Coffea canephora* (robusta) is more bitter and higher in caffeine.
- **Storage tip**: Keep beans in an airtight container away from light, heat, and moisture; use within 2 weeks of roasting for peak flavor.

## Core Ideas
- **Brewing temperature matters**: Optimal extraction occurs between 195–205°F (90–96°C). Too hot causes bitterness; too cool under-extracts.
- **Grind size dictates method**: Use fine grind for espresso, medium for drip, and coarse for French press to control extraction time.
- **Coffee-to-water ratio is key**: A standard starting ratio is 1:16 (e.g., 30g coffee to 480g water) for a balanced cup.
- **Freshness has a timeline**: Use whole beans within 2–4 weeks of roast date and grind immediately before brewing for best flavor.
- **Water quality impacts taste**: Use filtered water; minerals help extraction, but chlorine or impurities create off-flavors.

## How To Learn
- **Taste 3+ brewing methods** (e.g., French press, pour-over, espresso) to compare flavor profiles and extraction differences.
- **Measure coffee and water by weight** using a 1:16 ratio (e.g., 20g coffee to 320g water) for consistent strength.
- **Grind beans immediately before brewing** to preserve volatile aromatics; adjust grind size to control extraction time.
- **Use water at 90–96°C (195–205°F)**; too hot causes bitterness, too cool under-extracts.
- **Clean equipment after each use** to avoid stale oils altering flavor; descale machines monthly.

## Examples
- **Brewing ratio**: Use 60g coffee per 1L water (e.g., 18g for a 300ml pour-over) for balanced extraction.
- **Grind size**: Aim for a medium-fine grind (similar to table salt) for most drip methods to optimize flavor.
- **Water temperature**: Brew between 195–205°F (90–96°C); too hot causes bitterness, too cold under-extracts.
- **Bloom step**: Pour just enough water (twice the coffee's weight) to saturate grounds and wait 30 seconds to release CO₂.
- **Brew time**: Target 3–4 minutes for French press, 2–3 minutes for pour-over to avoid over-extraction.

## Summary
- A standard cup contains 240ml (8oz), with a typical brewed coffee providing 95mg of caffeine.
- The ideal brewing temperature is between 91–96°C (195–205°F) to extract optimal flavor without bitterness.
- Store beans in an airtight container away from light and heat; grind just before brewing for maximum freshness.
- For a balanced cup, use a 1:15 to 1:18 coffee-to-water ratio (e.g., 20g of coffee for 300ml of water).
- The four main roast profiles are Light, Medium, Medium-Dark, and Dark, each offering distinct flavor intensities.
