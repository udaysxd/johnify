## Ice Hockey Rules
- Games consist of three 20-minute periods with a 17-minute intermission after the first and second periods.
- A team must have no more than six players on the ice, including one goaltender, or they receive a minor penalty for too many men.
- Icing is called when a player shoots the puck from behind the center red line across the opponent's goal line without it being touched; play stops and a faceoff occurs in the offending team's zone.
- A minor penalty (e.g., tripping, slashing) results in the offending player serving 2 minutes in the penalty box, creating a power play for the opposing team.
- A goal is scored when the entire puck crosses the goal line between the posts and under the crossbar, either from a player's stick or by deflecting off a player.

## Core Ideas
- A team must have no more than six players on the ice, including one goaltender, or a minor penalty for too many players is assessed.
- Icing is called when a player shoots the puck from behind the center red line across the opponent's goal line without it being touched; the ensuing faceoff is in the offending team's defensive zone.
- A player is offside if they precede the puck into the offensive zone; play is stopped for a neutral zone faceoff.
- Minor penalties (e.g., tripping, slashing, hooking) result in a two-minute power play for the opposing team.
- A goal is scored when the entire puck crosses the entire goal line between the posts and under the crossbar.
- Overtime in the regular season is a five-minute, 3-on-3 sudden death period; if no goal is scored, a three-round shootout decides the winner.

## How To Learn
- Study the official IIHF rulebook, focusing on sections like Rule 10 (Penalties) and Rule 8 (Icing) for foundational knowledge.
- Watch professional games with a referee's perspective, noting specific calls like a 2-minute minor for tripping or the precise conditions for a waived-off icing.
- Join a local league's introductory clinic, which often includes on-ice sessions demonstrating offside positioning and proper face-off procedures.
- Use video analysis tools to break down game footage, identifying infractions such as a player entering the offensive zone before the puck (offside).
- Participate in online forums or communities dedicated to officiating to discuss nuanced rules, like the difference between a major and match penalty for checking from behind.
- Shadow an experienced referee during games to observe real-time decision-making, such as assessing a double-minor for high-sticking that causes injury.

## Examples
- **Icing**: Called when a player shoots the puck from behind the center red line across the opposing goal line without it being touched. Result: face-off in the defending zone of the team that committed the infraction.
- **Offside**: Occurs when an attacking player enters the offensive zone before the puck. The play is whistled dead, and a neutral zone face-off follows.
- **Minor Penalty (2 minutes)**: Infractions like tripping, slashing, or hooking. The penalized team plays short-handed until the time expires or a goal is scored.
- **Major Penalty (5 minutes)**: For serious offenses like fighting or boarding. The offending player serves a full 5 minutes, regardless of goals scored.
- **Delayed Offside**: Allows play to continue if all attacking players immediately exit the offensive zone upon realizing they are offside. If they touch the puck or interfere, offside is called.
- **Video Review**: Used to review goals for distinct kicking motions, puck entering the net before time expired, or goaltender interference. Coaches may challenge once per game; failed challenge results in a minor penalty for delay of game.

## Summary
- Games consist of three 20-minute periods of stop-time, with a 15-minute intermission after each period.
- A team is offside if any attacking player precedes the puck across the opposing team's blue line into the offensive zone.
- Icing is called when a player shoots the puck from behind the center red line across the opposing goal line without it being touched.
- Minor penalties (e.g., tripping, hooking, slashing) result in a 2-minute power play for the non-offending team.
- A goal is scored when the entire puck crosses the entire goal line between the posts and under the crossbar.
- Overtime in the regular season is a 5-minute, 3-on-3 sudden death period; playoffs feature continuous 20-minute periods until a goal is scored.
