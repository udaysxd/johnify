## Our Cosmic Neighbor: The Moon
- Earth's only natural satellite.
- A rocky, airless world.
- No light of its own, reflects sunlight.
- Its appearance changes from Earth.
- Drives our ocean tides.

<!-- NARRATION: Meet the Moon, our constant companion in space! It's a rocky, barren world that doesn't produce its own light but shines brightly by reflecting the Sun's rays. The Moon plays a crucial role in our planet's systems, most notably by influencing the tides of our oceans. -->

---

## Why the Moon Seems to Change
- The Moon orbits Earth.
- We see the Moon when sunlight hits it.
- The amount of sunlight we see changes.
- This is due to the Moon's position.
- The changing appearance is called phases.

<!-- NARRATION: Have you ever wondered why the Moon seems to change its shape in the sky? This is because the Moon orbits Earth, and we only see the parts of it that are illuminated by sunlight. As the Moon moves around us, the angle at which we view its sunlit surface changes, creating what we call the phases of the Moon. -->

---

## New Moon to Full Moon
- Starts with New Moon: Moon between Earth & Sun.
- Sunlit side faces away from us.
- Waxing Crescent: A sliver of light appears.
- First Quarter: Half of the Moon is lit.
- Waxing Gibbous: More than half lit.
- Full Moon: Earth is between Sun & Moon.

<!-- NARRATION: The Moon's journey through its phases begins with the New Moon, where it's hidden from our view because the sunlit side faces away from Earth. As it continues its orbit, we start to see a sliver of light in the Waxing Crescent, growing larger through the First Quarter and Waxing Gibbous, until we reach the spectacular Full Moon when the entire face we see is illuminated. -->

---

## Full Moon to New Moon
- Full Moon: Entire face is illuminated.
- Waning Gibbous: Light starts to decrease.
- Third Quarter: Opposite half of the Moon is lit.
- Waning Crescent: Light shrinks further.
- Back to New Moon, completing the cycle.

<!-- NARRATION: After the Full Moon, the process reverses. The Waning Gibbous phase sees the illuminated portion start to shrink, followed by the Third Quarter, where the opposite half of the Moon appears lit. The light continues to decrease through the Waning Crescent until the Moon once again becomes a New Moon, completing a full cycle. -->

---

## The Lunar Cycle
- A full cycle takes about 29.5 days.
- This is a synodic month.
- The phases are predictable.
- We always see the same side of the Moon.
- Understanding phases helps us chart time.

<!-- NARRATION: The entire process of the Moon's phases, from one New Moon to the next, takes approximately 29.5 days, a period known as a synodic month. These phases are a beautiful and predictable celestial dance, and learning about them helps us understand our place in the solar system and even track the passage of time. -->
