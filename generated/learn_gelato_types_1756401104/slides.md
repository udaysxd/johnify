## Gelato Types
- **Base Classification:** Primarily categorized into milk-based (e.g., fior di latte, chocolate, pistachio) and water-based (sorbetto, granita) varieties, with milk-based comprising approximately 70% of traditional production.
- **Key Ingredient Ratio:** Characterized by a lower butterfat content (typically 4–8%) compared to ice cream (14–25%), and less air overrun (20–30% vs. 50%+), resulting in a denser, more intense flavor.
- **Temperature Service:** Served at a slightly warmer temperature (−12°C to −14°C / 10°F to 6°F) than ice cream, which enhances flavor perception and creates a softer, silkier texture.
- **Regional Specialties:** Includes distinct regional variations such as Sicilian pistachio (Bronte DOP), Piedmont's hazelnut (Nocciola Piemonte IGP), and Venetian stracciatella.
- **Production Technique:** Traditionally churned slowly in a batch freezer to minimize air incorporation and maintain a creamy, dense consistency without hardening.

## Core Ideas
- Gelato contains 4–9% butterfat, significantly lower than ice cream's 14–25%, for a less fatty, more intense flavor.
- It is churned at a slower speed than ice cream, incorporating 25–30% less air (called overrun), resulting in a denser texture.
- Served at a warmer temperature, typically around 10–15°F (-12 to -9°C), which allows flavors to be more pronounced on the palate.
- Uses a higher proportion of milk to cream compared to ice cream, and often features a base of whole milk instead of heavy cream.
- Stabilizers like guar gum or carob bean gum are used in precise, small quantities (typically 0.1–0.5%) to bind water and prevent ice crystals.

## How To Learn
- **Taste 3–5 flavors per session** to avoid palate fatigue and distinguish subtle notes like vanilla bean specks versus bourbon-infused varieties.
- **Ask for a sample spoon** before committing to a full scoop; most gelaterias offer 1–2 tastings per customer to compare textures and sweetness.
- **Note the serving temperature** (-12°C to -14°C is ideal); if it melts too quickly or feels icy, it may contain stabilizers or be improperly churned.
- **Observe the color**: natural pistachio should be muted green (not bright), and banana should be greyish-white, not yellow, indicating real ingredients.
- **Prioritize seasonal batches** (e.g., strawberry in spring, persimmon in autumn) for peak flavor, as they often use ripe, local produce within 48 hours of harvesting.
- **Learn texture cues**: dense, elastic gelato has less air (overrun <35%), while fluffy, soft versions may have higher overrun (up to 60%), diluting flavor intensity.

## Examples
- **Fior di Latte**: Base flavor made with milk (3.25% fat), sugar (16-22% by weight), and no eggs, churned at -12°C for minimal air incorporation (≤35% overrun).  
- **Sorbetto**: Dairy-free, uses fruit purée (minimum 35% fruit content for citrus, 25% for others), sugar syrup, and stabilizers like pectin, served at -14°C.  
- **Semifreddo**: Contains whipped cream and meringue (egg-to-sugar ratio 1:2 by weight), frozen without churning, resulting in a soft, mousse-like texture.  
- **Gelato alla Crema**: Egg custard base (≥4 egg yolks per liter of milk), pasteurized at 65°C for 30 minutes, with ≤8% butterfat.  
- **Granita**: Coarse crystalline texture achieved by scraping during freezing; sugar concentration 15-20° Brix, served at -6°C.

## Summary
- Gelato contains 4–9% butterfat compared to ice cream's 14–25%, resulting in a denser, more intense flavor.
- It is churned at a slower speed, incorporating 25–30% less air, which prevents ice crystal formation for a silkier texture.
- Served at a warmer temperature, approximately 10–15°F (-12 to -9°C), to maintain its soft, scoopable consistency.
- Traditional flavor bases often start with a 'crema' (egg custard) or a 'latte' (milk) base, not a standard cream base.
- Popular artisanal flavors include Pistacchio di Bronte DOP, Fior di Latte, and Stracciatella with specific chocolate shard sizes.
