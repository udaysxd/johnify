## Ancient Rome Empire
- **Founding & Republic Era**: Established 753 BCE; Republic formed 509 BCE with Senate, Consuls, and Assemblies governing.
- **Imperial Expansion**: By 117 CE, empire spanned 5 million km² across Europe, Africa, Asia; population ~60–70 million.
- **Military Structure**: Professional army of ~300,000 legionaries; organized into legions of 5,000, using pilum, gladius, and testudo formation.
- **Engineering & Infrastructure**: Built 80,000 km of roads (e.g., Appian Way), aqueducts like Aqua Claudia supplying 1 million m³ water daily to Rome.
- **Legal System**: Twelve Tables (450 BCE) established written laws; later developed jus civile and jus gentium, influencing modern legal principles.
- **Decline Factors**: Divided into Eastern/Western empires 285 CE; fell in 476 due to economic crises, barbarian invasions, and political instability.

## Core Ideas
- **Citizenship Tiers**: Full citizens (c. 4-6 million by 1st century AD) had rights to vote, hold office, and marry legally; non-citizens (provincials, freedmen) had limited legal protections.
- **Pax Romana**: A 200-year period of relative peace (27 BC - 180 AD) enforced by legions, enabling vast trade networks spanning from Britain to Arabia.
- **"Bread and Circuses"**: The state provided free grain (dole for ~200,000 Romans) and public games (e.g., 150+ days of games annually) to maintain urban stability.
- **Roman Law Foundation**: Principles like "innocent until proven guilty" and the right to confront accusers were codified, forming the basis for many modern legal systems.

## How To Learn
- Read primary sources like Livy's *Ab Urbe Condita* and Suetonius's *The Twelve Caesars* to understand Roman perspectives.
- Memorize the major imperial dynasties (Julio-Claudian, Flavian, Nerva–Antonine, Severan) and their key rulers.
- Study the structure of a Roman legion (approx. 5,000 men, divided into 10 cohorts and centuries of 80 men).
- Analyze key battles (e.g., Battle of Cannae, 216 BCE; Battle of Actium, 31 BCE) to understand military tactics.
- Trace the major trade routes (e.g., the Via Appia, grain supply from Egypt) to grasp economic foundations.
- Visit archaeological sites like the Roman Forum or Pompeii to contextualize architectural and daily life evidence.

## Examples
- **Aqueducts**: The Aqua Claudia delivered 190,000 cubic meters of water daily to Rome over 68 km.
- **Roads**: The 535 km Via Appia, begun in 312 BCE, connected Rome to Brundisium (Brindisi).
- **Legal Code**: The Law of the Twelve Tables (c. 450 BCE) established public rights and procedures.
- **Military**: A legion contained ~5,000 men, organized into 10 cohorts of 6 centuries each.
- **Concrete**: Used volcanic ash (pozzolana) to create hydraulic concrete for harbors like Portus.

## Summary
- Founded in 27 BCE by Augustus, the empire lasted over 500 years until the fall of the Western Empire in 476 CE.
- At its peak under Trajan (117 CE), it spanned 5 million km², from Britain to Egypt, with an estimated 70 million inhabitants.
- Governed by a single emperor (Princeps) who controlled the military, appointed senators, and served as Pontifex Maximus.
- Maintained control through a professional standing army of ~300,000 legionaries and an extensive network of roads spanning 400,000 km.
- Its legacy includes Latin-derived languages, Roman law principles, and architectural innovations like concrete and the arch.
