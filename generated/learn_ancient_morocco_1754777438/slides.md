## Introduction to ancient morocco
- **Ancient Morocco** refers to the historical period spanning from prehistoric times to the early Islamic era, featuring diverse cultures like the **Berbers, Phoenicians, and Romans**.
- It’s **culturally and historically significant** as a crossroads of African, Mediterranean, and Arab civilizations, shaping trade, art, and politics.
- **Fun fact:** The ancient city of Volubilis, a Roman ruin in Morocco, was once a thriving olive oil producer, exporting over **1 million liters annually**.

## Slide 2
- **Phoenician and Carthaginian Influence**: The Phoenicians and later Carthaginians established **trading posts** along Morocco's coast, introducing new goods and cultural exchanges. This matters because it connected Morocco to wider Mediterranean trade networks.
- **Roman Province of Mauretania**: Morocco became part of the **Roman Empire** as Mauretania Tingitana, with cities like Volubilis showcasing Roman architecture. This matters because it left lasting urban and cultural legacies in the region.
- **Berber Kingdoms and Resistance**: Indigenous **Berber kingdoms**, like the Mauri, resisted foreign rule while adopting some outside influences. This matters because it highlights Morocco's long history of cultural blending and independence.

## Examples of ancient morocco
- **Volubilis**
- A well-preserved **Roman city** in northern Morocco, founded in the 3rd century BCE.
- Its ruins showcase **Roman architecture**, mosaics, and urban planning, highlighting Morocco's role in the **Roman Empire**.
