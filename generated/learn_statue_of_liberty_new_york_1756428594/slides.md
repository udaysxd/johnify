## Statue Of Liberty New York
- Gift from France in 1886, designed by Frédéric Auguste Bartholdi with internal framework by Gustave Eiffel
- Stands 305 feet (93 meters) tall from base to torch, weighing approximately 450,000 pounds (204,100 kg)
- Located on Liberty Island in New York Harbor, accessible by ferry from Battery Park or Liberty State Park
- Features a broken chain at the feet, a tablet inscribed "JULY IV MDCCLXXVI," and a seven-ray crown representing the seven continents
- Visitors can climb 354 steps to the crown (reservations required months in advance) or explore the pedestal museum
- Serves as a UNESCO World Heritage Site and a universal symbol of freedom and democracy

## Core Ideas
- Gift from France in 1886 to commemorate the U.S. centennial and celebrate shared democratic ideals.
- Stands 305 feet (93 meters) tall from the base to the tip of the torch, with a copper exterior that has oxidized to its iconic green patina.
- Designed by Frédéric Auguste Bartholdi, with the internal iron framework engineered by Gustave Eiffel.
- Located on Liberty Island in New York Harbor, accessible only by a 15-minute ferry ride from Battery Park.
- The statue's tablet is inscribed with "JULY IV MDCCLXXVI" (July 4, 1776), the date of the U.S. Declaration of Independence.
- Serves as a universal symbol of freedom and democracy, welcoming over 4 million visitors annually.

## How To Learn
- **Visit the National Park Service website** to reserve ferry tickets and pedestal access up to 6 months in advance, as walk-up tickets are extremely limited.
- **Study the statue's history and symbolism** before your visit; understand that the 7 rays on her crown represent the 7 continents and oceans, and the broken chains at her feet symbolize freedom.
- **Use the official audio tour app** for a self-guided experience; it provides 45 minutes of detailed commentary keyed to 25 specific locations on Liberty Island.
- **Plan for a 4–5 hour visit minimum**, accounting for security screening, ferry travel (approx. 15 minutes each way), and exploring both the museum and grounds.

## Examples
- Gift from France in 1886: 151-foot copper statue on 89-foot pedestal, total height 305 feet from ground to torch tip.
- Construction: Designed by Frédéric Bartholdi, with internal iron framework engineered by Gustave Eiffel.
- Symbolism: Broken chains at feet represent freedom from oppression; tablet inscribed "JULY IV MDCCLXXVI" (July 4, 1776).
- Restoration (1984–1986): $87 million project replaced original torch with gold-leaf covered copper replica.
- Visitor access: Crown access requires reservation for 162-step climb; pedestal museum accessible with general admission.

## Summary
- Gift from France in 1886, commemorating the centennial of the American Declaration of Independence and the alliance between the two nations.
- Stands 305 feet (93 meters) tall from the base to the tip of the torch, with the copper statue itself measuring 151 feet (46 meters).
- Located on Liberty Island in New York Harbor, accessible only by approved ferry service from Battery Park or Liberty State Park.
- The statue's full name is "Liberty Enlightening the World," and it symbolizes freedom and democracy.
- Visitors can tour the pedestal and museum with a standard ticket, but crown access requires a separate, limited-availability reservation booked months in advance.
