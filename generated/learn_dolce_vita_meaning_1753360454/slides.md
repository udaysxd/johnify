## Introduction to dolce vita meaning
- **Dolce vita** is an Italian phrase meaning **"the sweet life"**—a lifestyle focused on **pleasure, leisure, and indulgence**, often associated with post-war Italy.
- It’s culturally significant as it **captures a timeless ideal** of enjoying life’s luxuries, influencing art, film (like Fellini’s *La Dolce Vita*), and modern wellness trends.
- Fun fact: The term saw a **300% spike in online searches** after the 2020 lockdowns, reflecting a global craving for slower, more joyful living.

## Slide 2
- **Italian Origin**: The phrase **"dolce vita"** translates to **"sweet life"** in Italian, symbolizing a life of pleasure, leisure, and indulgence. It matters because it captures a cultural ideal of enjoying life’s luxuries and simple joys.
- **Cinematic Influence**: The term became globally famous after Federico Fellini’s 1960 film *La Dolce Vita*, which depicted glamorous but shallow post-war Rome. This matters because it shaped modern perceptions of **luxury, fame, and existential searching**.
- **Lifestyle Association**: Today, "dolce vita" represents a **carefree, stylish way of living**, often linked to travel, fine dining, and relaxation. It matters as it inspires people to seek balance and enjoyment in their daily lives.
