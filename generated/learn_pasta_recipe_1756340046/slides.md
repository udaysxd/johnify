- **Objective**: Master the techniques for making fresh pasta from scratch
- **Key principles**: Proper dough hydration, gluten development, and shaping
- **Essential tools**: Mixing bowl, scale, rolling pin or pasta machine, sharp knife

## The Dough: Mixing & Kneading
- **Weigh ingredients** for precision (100g flour per egg)
- Create a flour well, add eggs to center, gradually incorporate
- **Knead vigorously** for 8-10 minutes until smooth and elastic
- Rest dough wrapped for 30 minutes to relax gluten

<!-- NARRATION: Let's make our pasta dough! Always weigh your ingredients for precision—use 100g of flour per egg. Create a flour well, crack eggs into the center, and gradually incorporate the flour. Now knead vigorously for 8-10 minutes until the dough becomes smooth and elastic. Finally, wrap it and let it rest for 30 minutes. This resting period relaxes the gluten, making the dough easier to roll and shape later. -->

## Classic Shape Variations
- **Tagliatelle**: 6-8mm wide ribbons, ideal for meat sauces
- **Fettuccine**: Slightly wider than tagliatelle, versatile
- **Pappardelle**: 2-3cm wide, perfect for hearty ragù
- **Filled pastas**: Ravioli, tortellini require precise sealing

<!-- NARRATION: Now for the fun part—shaping! Classic ribbon pastas include tagliatelle, which are 6-8mm wide and perfect with meat sauces. Fettuccine are slightly wider and very versatile. Pappardelle are broad, 2-3cm ribbons ideal for hearty ragù. If you're feeling adventurous, try filled pastas like ravioli or tortellini. Remember, sealing filled pastas properly is crucial to prevent them from opening during cooking. Each shape offers a different eating experience! -->

## Sauce Pairing Principles
- **Light shapes** (angel hair) pair with delicate sauces
- **Ribbon pastas** hold creamy or oil-based sauces well
- **Tube shapes** capture chunky sauces internally
- **Stuffed pastas** work with butter or light tomato sauces

<!-- NARRATION: Pairing pasta with the right sauce is key to a great dish. Delicate shapes like angel hair work best with light, thin sauces. Ribbon pastas such as fettuccine hold creamy or oil-based sauces beautifully. Tube shapes like penne capture chunky sauces inside. For stuffed pastas, stick to simple butter or light tomato sauces that won't overpower the filling. Matching shape to sauce ensures every bite is perfectly balanced. -->

## Advanced Techniques & Variations
- **Colored pasta**: Spinach (green), beetroot (pink), squid ink (black)
- **Egg-free options**: Semolina and water dough
- **Flavor infusions**: Herb- or spice-incorporated dough
- **Resting variations**: Overnight refrigeration for deeper flavor

<!-- NARRATION: Ready to get creative? Try colored pasta by adding spinach for green, beetroot for pink, or squid ink for black. For egg-free options, use semolina and water. Infuse flavors directly into the dough with herbs or spices. You can even refrigerate the dough overnight to develop deeper flavors. These advanced techniques let you customize your pasta, making each batch unique and tailored to your taste preferences. -->
