## Introduction to shark tank
- **Shark Tank** is a reality television show where entrepreneurs pitch their businesses to a panel of wealthy investors, called "sharks," who decide whether to invest their own money
- The show is important because it demonstrates **real-world business negotiations** and teaches valuable lessons about entrepreneurship, valuation, and deal-making
- Since its premiere, the sharks have invested over **$200 million** in various businesses across more than 800 deals on the show

## Key Concepts
- **Valuation** determines what percentage of the company an entrepreneur trades for an investment, which matters because it sets the financial terms of the deal and directly impacts the ownership stake of both the founders and the Sharks.
- **Equity** is the ownership share an entrepreneur offers to an investor in exchange for capital and expertise, and it is crucial because it dictates who controls the company's future decisions and profits.

## How shark tank Works
- **Sharks ask probing questions**: Investors grill entrepreneurs about finances, valuation, patents, and scalability to uncover risks and opportunities. This due diligence is vital because it reveals the true health of the business and whether it's worth investing in.

## Examples of shark tank
- **Scrub Daddy** - A smiley-faced cleaning sponge that changes texture based on water temperature, becoming firm in cold water and soft in warm water. This product demonstrates how **patent protection** and strong branding can create a defensible market position that attracts Shark investment.
- **Bombas** - Socks designed with honeycomb architecture for comfort, with a buy-one-donate-one social mission model. This illustrates how combining **product innovation** with a strong **social entrepreneurship** angle can create both commercial success and positive brand recognition.

## Summary: shark tank
- **Entrepreneurs pitch to multiple investors simultaneously**, creating a competitive environment where sharks must quickly assess value and potential. This dynamic often leads to intense negotiations and strategic bidding wars that can dramatically increase a company's valuation or result in better deal terms for the entrepreneur.
- **Sharks invest their own personal capital** rather than institutional funds, making them highly selective and personally invested in each deal's success. This creates authentic stakes where their business expertise and network connections become as valuable as the financial investment itself, providing entrepreneurs with mentorship and growth opportunities beyond capital.
- **Deals often include equity stakes and royalty agreements**, combining ownership shares with ongoing revenue-based payments to mitigate investor risk. This hybrid approach allows sharks to recover investments faster while still participating in long-term growth, demonstrating sophisticated financial structuring that balances immediate returns with future upside potential.
