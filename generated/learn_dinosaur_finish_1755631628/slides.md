## Introduction to dinosaur finish
- **Dinosaur finish** refers to the final evolutionary adaptations and characteristics of dinosaurs just before their mass extinction 66 million years ago
- Understanding these late-stage features helps paleontologists reconstruct the **final ecosystems** dinosaurs inhabited and their responses to environmental pressures
- Fossil evidence shows that **75% of known dinosaur species** from this period were still thriving and diversifying right up to the asteroid impact event

## Key Concepts
- **Extinction Event**: The Cretaceous-Paleogene extinction event, likely caused by an asteroid impact, wiped out all non-avian dinosaurs about 66 million years ago, which matters because it ended the dinosaurs' 165-million-year reign and allowed mammals to eventually dominate Earth.
- **Surviving Lineage**: While most dinosaurs went extinct, their theropod relatives evolved into modern birds, which matters because it shows dinosaurs didn't completely disappear but continue living among us as over 10,000 bird species today.
- **Fossil Record**: The sudden disappearance of dinosaur fossils in rock layers marks the end of the Cretaceous period, which matters because it provides clear geological evidence of mass extinction and helps scientists date evolutionary events in Earth's history.

## Examples of dinosaur finish
- **Tyrannosaurus rex** fossils often show **osteomyelitis**, a severe bone infection that left visible lesions and deformities. This reveals how large predators survived serious injuries and provides insight into dinosaur immune responses and healing capabilities.
- **Triceratops** skulls frequently display **healed combat wounds** including punctures and fractures that match their own horn shapes. These injuries demonstrate **intraspecific combat** behaviors and show how these dinosaurs engaged in ritualized fighting for dominance or mating rights.
- **Edmontosaurus** specimens commonly exhibit **bite marks and tooth drags** from tyrannosaur attacks that show clear signs of healing. These injuries provide direct evidence of predator-prey relationships and prove some dinosaurs survived initial attacks through effective escape or defensive behaviors.

## Summary: dinosaur finish
- **Mass extinction event** wiped out all non-avian dinosaurs 66 million years ago, likely caused by an asteroid impact and volcanic activity. This demonstrates how sudden catastrophic events can permanently alter Earth's ecosystems and shows why planetary defense against asteroids remains scientifically important today.
- The extinction created **ecological vacancies** that allowed mammals to diversify and eventually dominate terrestrial ecosystems. This pivotal shift explains why mammals rather than dinosaurs became Earth's dominant land animals and illustrates how major extinction events can create opportunities for new life forms to emerge and evolve.
- Some dinosaur lineages survived as modern **birds**, making them the only living dinosaur descendants. This continuity shows that not all dinosaurs went completely extinct and highlights how evolutionary relationships connect ancient creatures to modern species, changing our perspective on what constitutes a "living dinosaur."
