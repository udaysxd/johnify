## What Are Prime Numbers?
- **Prime numbers** are natural numbers greater than 1 that have no positive divisors other than 1 and themselves.
- Examples: 2, 3, 5, 7, 11, 13, 17, 19, 23, etc.
- Non-examples: 4 (divisible by 2), 6 (divisible by 2 and 3), 9 (divisible by 3).

## How to Check for Primality
- For small numbers: test divisibility by primes up to the square root of the number.
- Example: To check if 29 is prime, test divisibility by primes ≤ √29 (i.e., 2, 3, 5).
  - 29 ÷ 2 = 14.5 (not integer)
  - 29 ÷ 3 ≈ 9.67 (not integer)
  - 29 ÷ 5 = 5.8 (not integer)
  - So, 29 is prime.

## Importance of Prime Numbers
- Foundation of number theory.
- Crucial in cryptography (e.g., RSA encryption relies on large primes).
- Used in hashing algorithms and random number generation.
- Helpful in optimizing algorithms and mathematical proofs.

## Common Misconceptions
- 1 is not a prime number (it has only one positive divisor).
- Not all odd numbers are primes (e.g., 15, 21, 27).
- Primes are not always isolated; some appear close together (twin primes).
