## Introduction to ancient silk route
- The **Ancient Silk Route** was a vast network of trade routes connecting **East Asia to the Mediterranean**, active from around **130 BCE to 1453 CE**. It facilitated the exchange of goods, ideas, and cultures.
- This route was **crucial for global trade**, enabling the spread of **silk, spices, technology, and religions** like Buddhism and Islam across continents.
- An interesting fact: At its peak, the Silk Route spanned over **4,000 miles**, with some caravans taking **years** to complete their journeys.

## How ancient silk route Works
- **Trade Begins in China**: Chinese merchants **produced silk, porcelain, and spices**, then transported them westbound. This started the exchange of **luxury goods** and ideas across continents.
- **Goods Travel Along Routes**: Caravans and ships carried goods through **Central Asia, India, and the Middle East**, with traders stopping at bustling **market cities** like Samarkand. This connected distant economies and cultures.
- **Goods Reach Europe**: Merchants in **Mediterranean ports** (like Constantinople) sold Asian goods to European buyers. This **spread wealth, technology, and cultural influences** across the ancient world.
