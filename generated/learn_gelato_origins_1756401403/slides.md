## The Origins of Gelato
- **Gelato**: Italian-style ice cream known for its dense texture and intense flavor.
- Originated in **Italy**, with roots tracing back to the **Renaissance**.
- Often confused with ice cream, but differs in fat content, air incorporation, and serving temperature.

<!-- NARRATION: Welcome to the world of gelato! This Italian-style ice cream is famous for its rich, dense texture and bold flavors. It all began in Italy during the Renaissance, and while it's often mistaken for regular ice cream, gelato has less fat, less air, and is served at a slightly warmer temperature. Understanding these differences helps us appreciate why gelato feels so smooth and flavourful on the palate. -->

## Key Innovations
- Introduction of the **sorbettiere** (gelato maker) in the **17th century** by Sicilian fisherman **Procopio Cutò**.
- Cutò opened **Café Procope** in Paris, introducing gelato to France and broader Europe.
- Advancements in **refrigeration technology** in the 19th and 20th centuries made production more efficient.

<!-- NARRATION: Key innovations really shaped gelato's history. In the 17th century, a Sicilian fisherman named Procopio Cutò invented the sorbettiere, a machine for making gelato. ## Regional Variations in Italy
- **Northern Italy**: Often uses more milk and eggs, creating a creamier base.
- **Southern Italy**: Tends to favor fruit-based, water-rich recipes (like sorbets).
- Sicily famous for **granita**, a related frozen dessert with a coarse, icy texture.

<!-- NARRATION: Italy's regions each put their own spin on gelato! In the north, recipes often include more milk and eggs, giving a creamier, richer base. These regional differences highlight Italy's diverse culinary traditions and local ingredients. -->

## Global Spread and Evolution
- Italian immigrants brought gelato to **North and South America** in the late 19th and early 20th centuries.
- **Artisanal gelato movement** revived traditional methods in the late 20th century.
- Today, gelato is enjoyed worldwide, with both classic and innovative flavors.

<!-- NARRATION: Gelato didn't stay in Italy—it went global! Italian immigrants brought it to the Americas in the late 1800s and early 1900s. Today, you can find gelato everywhere, with both classic flavours and creative new ones, showing how this dessert has evolved while staying true to its roots. -->

## Cultural Significance
- Gelato is a staple of **Italian daily life and celebration**.
- **Gelaterias** are social hubs in Italian towns and cities.
- Represents **Italian craftsmanship and culinary artistry**.
