## Cookies Australian
- **Anzac Biscuits**: Oat-based cookies with golden syrup, no eggs, created during WWI for long shelf life to send to troops.
- **Tim Tam Slam**: Bite opposite corners of a Tim Tam, use it as a straw for hot coffee or milk, then eat the melted biscuit.
- **Iced VoVo**: Pink fondant and raspberry jam on a rectangular biscuit, topped with coconut strips; a classic from Arnott's since 1906.
- **Monte Carlo**: Two malt biscuit layers sandwiching raspberry jam and vanilla cream; over 40 million sold annually by Arnott's.
- **Pavlova Magic**: Crushed meringue folded into whipped cream and fruit, often served as a dessert, not a traditional cookie but a popular treat.

## Core Ideas
- **ANZAC Biscuits**: Originated during WWI, made without eggs for long shelf life; key ingredients are rolled oats, golden syrup, and desiccated coconut.
- **Tim Tam Slam**: Iconic eating method for Australia's favourite biscuit; bite off opposite corners and use it as a straw to drink milk or coffee.
- **Iced VoVo**: Classic Arnott's biscuit featuring a strip of raspberry jam and two strips of pink fondant, topped with coconut.
- **Recipe Standard**: Most traditional recipes use grams for measurement; a standard batch makes approximately 24 biscuits.
- **Baking Tip**: For chewy biscuits, remove from oven when lightly golden; for crunchier ones, bake for 2-3 extra minutes.

## How To Learn
- **Practice spaced repetition**: Review cookie recipes every 2–3 days to reinforce memory, like revisiting Anzac biscuit ingredients on days 1, 3, and 7.
- **Apply hands-on baking**: Learn by doing—measure 250g flour, cream 150g butter with sugar, and note texture changes to understand ratios and techniques.
- **Analyze failed batches**: If cookies spread too much, test reducing butter by 10% or chilling dough for 30 minutes; document adjustments for improvement.
- **Study expert recipes**: Compare 3 versions of Tim Tam-inspired cookies to identify common techniques, such as rolling dough to 1cm thickness for even baking.
- **Join communities**: Share photos on r/AustralianBaking for feedback on browning or chewiness, and adapt tips like adding macadamia nuts for crunch.

## Examples
- **Anzac Biscuits**: Oat-based cookies using golden syrup, no eggs, originally sent to WWI soldiers; bake 15–18 minutes at 160°C.
- **Tim Tams**: Chocolate-coated sandwich biscuits with malted filling; 11 biscuits per 200g pack, often used for "Tim Tam Slams".
- **Iced VoVos**: Pink fondant and raspberry jam on a biscuit base, topped with coconut; made by Arnott's since 1906.
- **Monte Carlos**: Two biscuits with raspberry jam and cream filling; typical recipe uses 125g butter and ½ cup brown sugar.
- **Choc Chip Cookies**: Often include macadamia nuts; Aussie versions may use Vegemite for a salty twist.

## Summary
- Australian cookies often feature native ingredients like wattleseed, macadamia nuts, or lemon myrtle.
- Iconic examples include Anzac biscuits (rolled oats, golden syrup) and Tim Tams (chocolate malted biscuits).
- A key baking tip is to chill the dough for at least 30 minutes to prevent excessive spreading.
- Popular recipes often use the metric system, with temperatures around 160–180°C (320–350°F).
- They are commonly enjoyed with a cup of tea or coffee, often as a morning or afternoon snack.
