## Introduction to Plastics
- **Plastics**: synthetic or semi-synthetic materials made from polymers
- Key properties: lightweight, durable, moldable, and resistant to moisture/chemicals
- Global production exceeds 400 million tons annually
- Classification by **recycling codes** (Resin Identification Codes 1–7)

## Polyethylene Terephthalate (PET/PETE #1)
- **Properties**: clear, strong, lightweight, excellent barrier to gases/moisture
- Common uses: beverage bottles, food containers, synthetic fibers
- Highly recyclable; often turned into new bottles or polyester fiber
- Limitations: susceptible to degradation at high temperatures

<!-- NARRATION: Let's look at PET, or plastic number 1. It's clear, strong, and lightweight, making it perfect for drink bottles and food containers. Next time you recycle, check for that number 1 symbol to help reduce waste! -->

## Polyvinyl Chloride (PVC #3)
- **Properties**: rigid or flexible (with plasticizers), durable, weather resistant
- Common uses: piping, window frames, medical tubing, flooring
- Recycling is challenging due to additives; often downcycled
- Environmental and health concerns: may release toxins when burned or landfilled

<!-- NARRATION: Now, onto PVC, labeled as number 3. It can be rigid or flexible, used in things like pipes and medical tubing. It's important to handle and dispose of PVC carefully to protect our health and planet. -->

## Polypropylene (PP #5)
- **Properties**: high melting point, fatigue resistant, semi-rigid
- Common uses: food containers, automotive parts, textiles, medical devices
- Recyclable and increasingly collected in programs
- Resistant to many chemical solvents, acids, and bases

<!-- NARRATION: Meet polypropylene, or PP, number 5. It has a high melting point and resists fatigue, making it great for food containers, car parts, and medical devices. PP is recyclable and more programs are accepting it now. Plus, it stands up well to many chemicals. Choosing products made from PP can be a smart, sustainable choice thanks to its durability and recyclability. -->

## Other Plastics (Miscellaneous #7)
- Includes **polycarbonate (PC)**, **polylactic acid (PLA)**, and multi-material composites
- **Polycarbonate**: strong, transparent; used in eyewear, electronics
- **PLA**: biodegradable, made from renewable resources; used in compostable packaging
- Recycling challenges: mixed materials complicate processing

## Summary and Key Takeaways
- Plastics categorized by resin codes (#1–7), each with distinct properties and uses
- **Thermoplastics** are recyclable; **thermosets** are not
- Environmental impacts include pollution, toxicity, and persistence
- Responsible use: reduce single-use, recycle correctly, support sustainable alternatives
