## Introduction to jazz music
- **Jazz music** is a **dynamic genre** born in the early 20th century, blending **African rhythms, blues, and improvisation** to create expressive, ever-evolving soundscapes.
- Jazz is **culturally significant** as a cornerstone of American music history, influencing genres like **rock, hip-hop, and R&B** while celebrating creativity and collaboration.
- **Fun fact:** Over **60% of jazz musicians** cite improvisation as the genre's defining feature, making no two performances exactly alike.

## Slide 2
- **Improvisation**: Jazz musicians often **create music spontaneously** during performances, building on melodies and harmonies in real time. This matters because it makes each performance unique and showcases the artist's creativity.
- **Syncopation**: Jazz frequently uses **off-beat rhythms**, emphasizing unexpected parts of the musical measure. This matters because it gives jazz its distinctive, lively, and unpredictable feel.
- **Call and Response**: Jazz features a musical conversation where one instrument or voice **"calls"** and another **"responds."** This matters because it creates dynamic interaction between musicians, adding depth and energy to the music.

## How jazz music Works
- **Improvisation**: Jazz musicians **create melodies on the spot** over a song's chord progression. This makes each performance unique and showcases individual creativity.
- **Swing Rhythm**: Jazz uses a **syncopated, uneven rhythm** that emphasizes off-beats. This gives jazz its signature groove and lively, unpredictable feel.
- **Call and Response**: Musicians **trade phrases** with each other, mimicking or replying to ideas. This builds musical conversation and keeps the performance dynamic.
