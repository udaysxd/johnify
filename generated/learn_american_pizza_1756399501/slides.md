## American Pizza
- New York-style pizza features a thin, foldable crust baked at 550°F (288°C) and is typically sold in 18-inch (46 cm) slices.
- Chicago deep-dish pizza uses a 2–3 inch (5–7.6 cm) high pan, requires 30–45 minutes to bake, and layers cheese directly on the crust below the sauce.
- A standard large pepperoni pizza uses approximately 40–50 slices of pepperoni and 2–3 cups (200–300g) of shredded mozzarella cheese.
- To prevent a soggy crust, preheat the oven for at least 30 minutes and use a pizza stone or steel heated to 500°F (260°C).
- Common regional variations include Detroit-style (rectangular with crispy cheese edges), California-style (gourmet, non-traditional toppings), and St. Louis-style (cracker-thin crust with Provel cheese).

## Core Ideas
- **Thin, foldable crust** distinguishes New York-style pizza, baked at 550°F (288°C) for 4–6 minutes
- **Deep-dish pizza** requires a 2–3 inch high pan, 45-minute bake time, and cheese layered *below* the sauce
- **Topping distribution** follows the 40/30/30 rule: 40% cheese, 30% sauce, 30% toppings by coverage area
- **Proofing dough** is critical: 24–72 hours cold fermentation develops flavor and creates airy cornicione
- **Regional styles** include Detroit (rectangular, crispy cheese edge), St. Louis (cracker-thin, Provel cheese), and California (gourmet ingredients)

## How To Learn
- **Study the 3 core styles**: Master New York (thin, foldable crust), Chicago (deep-dish, layered toppings), and Neapolitan (soft, wood-fired crust) to understand foundational differences.
- **Practice dough hydration**: Aim for 60–65% hydration for New York style, and 70–80% for Neapolitan, measuring flour and water by weight for consistency.
- **Use a baking steel or stone**: Preheat at 500°F (260°C) for at least 45 minutes to achieve a crisp, professional-quality crust at home.
- **Learn topping balance**: Apply sauce (⅓ cup), cheese (4 oz), and toppings (2–3 oz) in even layers to prevent sogginess and ensure even cooking.
- **Experiment with fermentation**: Cold-ferment dough for 24–72 hours to develop complex flavor and improve texture before baking.

## Examples
- **New York-style**: Thin, foldable crust (approx. 18" diameter) baked at 550°F (288°C) in deck ovens, typically topped with low-moisture mozzarella and tomato sauce with a high gluten flour blend.
- **Chicago deep-dish**: Thick, pie-like crust (2–3" tall) baked in round steel pans, layered with cheese, toppings, and chunky tomato sauce on top, requiring 30–45 minutes baking time.
- **Detroit-style**: Rectangular pan pizza with thick, airy crust, crispy cheese-edged "frico" crust, topped with Wisconsin brick cheese and sauce applied in stripes post-baking.
- **Neapolitan (American interpretation)**: Wood-fired at 800–900°F (427–482°C) for 60–90 seconds, featuring charred cornicione, San Marzano tomatoes, and fresh mozzarella di bufala.
- **California-style**: Thin crust with non-traditional toppings like barbecue chicken, arugula, or goat cheese, often using organic ingredients and whole wheat crust options.
- **St. Louis-style**: Ultra-thin cracker-like crust without yeast, cut into squares, topped with Provel processed cheese and sweet, oregano-heavy tomato sauce.

## Summary
- New York-style pizza is characterized by its thin, foldable crust and large slices, typically 18–20 inches in diameter.
- Chicago deep-dish pizza is baked in a round steel pan, with a thick crust up to 3 inches high and ingredients in reverse order (cheese first, then toppings, then sauce).
- Authentic Neapolitan pizza, as defined by VPN association rules, must be cooked in a wood-fired oven at 905°F (485°C) for 60–90 seconds.
- A standard American pepperoni pizza uses approximately 40–50 slices of pepperoni and 8–12 ounces of low-moisture, whole-milk mozzarella cheese.
- To prevent a soggy center when making pizza at home, pre-bake the crust for 3–4 minutes at 475°F (245°C) before adding sauce and toppings.
