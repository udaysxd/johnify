## Albert Einstein
- Developed the theory of general relativity in 1915, predicting gravitational waves and the bending of light by gravity
- Published the photoelectric effect paper in 1905, introducing light quanta (photons) and earning the 1921 Nobel Prize in Physics
- Derived the mass-energy equivalence formula E=mc², showing that 1 gram of mass contains 8.99×10¹³ joules of energy
- Proposed the Einstein-Podolsky-Rosen paradox in 1935, challenging quantum mechanics' completeness and inspiring entanglement research
- Fled Nazi Germany in 1933, immigrated to the United States, and took a position at Princeton's Institute for Advanced Study
- Authored over 300 scientific papers and 150 non-scientific works, with an estimated IQ between 160-180

## Core Ideas
- **Special Relativity (1905):** Introduced the principle that the laws of physics are identical in all inertial frames, and the speed of light in a vacuum (c = 299,792,458 m/s) is constant for all observers.
- **Mass-Energy Equivalence:** Formulated the equation E=mc², demonstrating that mass and energy are interchangeable; a tiny amount of mass can be converted into a vast amount of energy.
- **General Relativity (1915):** Proposed that gravity is not a force but a curvature of spacetime caused by mass and energy, accurately predicting the bending of light by the Sun (1.75 arcseconds).
- **Photoelectric Effect:** Explained that light consists of quantized packets of energy (photons), with electron emission dependent on light frequency, not intensity, earning him the 1921 Nobel Prize.
- **Unified Field Theory:** Spent decades attempting to unify gravity and electromagnetism into a single theoretical framework, though it remained incomplete.

## How To Learn
- **Embrace Curiosity-Driven Exploration**: Einstein attributed his breakthroughs to persistent questioning, not intelligence. Spend at least 30 minutes daily exploring tangential questions unrelated to your immediate goals.
- **Master the Fundamentals Before Innovating**: He mastered Maxwell’s equations and classical mechanics before developing relativity. Dedicate 70% of study time to solidifying core principles in your field before attempting original work.
- **Utilize Thought Experiments for Conceptual Testing**: Einstein visualized chasing a light beam to develop special relativity. Regularly practice constructing and analyzing detailed mental models to challenge and refine assumptions.
- **Collaborate and Debate with Critical Peers**: His discussions with Michele Besso and the Olympia Academy were crucial. Form a study group of 3–5 people with diverse expertise to rigorously critique each other’s ideas weekly.
- **Simplify Complex Ideas into Core Principles**: He reduced gravity to spacetime curvature. After learning a concept, practice explaining it in one paragraph without jargon, ensuring you grasp its essence.

## Examples
- **Special Relativity (1905):** Introduced the equation \(E = mc^2\), demonstrating that mass and energy are interchangeable, with energy (E) equal to mass (m) multiplied by the speed of light squared (\(c^2 ≈ 9 × 10^{16} m^2/s^2\)).
- **Photoelectric Effect (1905):** Explained that light consists of discrete packets of energy (photons), with each photon's energy calculated as \(E = hf\), where \(h\) is Planck's constant (\(6.626 × 10^{-34} J·s\)) and \(f\) is frequency.
- **General Relativity (1915):** Proposed that gravity results from the curvature of spacetime by mass, famously confirmed by the observed 1.75 arcseconds bending of starlight during the 1919 solar eclipse.
- **Brownian Motion (1905):** Provided empirical evidence for the existence of atoms by mathematically modeling the random motion of pollen particles in water, linking microscopic collisions to observable motion.

## Summary
- Developed the theory of special relativity in 1905, introducing the equation E=mc² and the concept that the speed of light is constant for all observers.
- Published the general theory of relativity in 1915, describing gravity as the curvature of spacetime caused by mass and energy.
- Won the 1921 Nobel Prize in Physics for his explanation of the photoelectric effect, which demonstrated the particle nature of light.
- Proposed the cosmological constant (Λ) in 1917 as a repulsive force to allow a static universe, later calling it his "biggest blunder" after the discovery of the universe's expansion.
- Advocated for pacifism and civil rights, and co-authored the Russell–Einstein Manifesto in 1955 warning of the dangers of nuclear weapons.
