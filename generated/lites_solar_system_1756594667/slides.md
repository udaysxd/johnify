## Our Solar System: A Cosmic Neighborhood
- A star (the Sun) and everything gravitationally bound to it.
- Planets, dwarf planets, moons, asteroids, and comets.
- Formed about 4.6 billion years ago from a giant cloud of gas and dust.
- Located in the Milky Way galaxy.

## The Sun: The Heart of Our System
- A medium-sized star, a ball of hot plasma.
- Provides light and heat essential for life on Earth.
- Fusion reactions in its core generate immense energy.
- Accounts for over 99% of the solar system's mass.
- Drives weather and climate on planets.

<!-- NARRATION: At the heart of it all is the Sun, our very own star. It's a giant ball of gas that provides the light and heat we need to survive, and its energy shapes everything in our cosmic neighborhood. -->

## The Inner Planets: Rocky Worlds
- Mercury, Venus, Earth, and Mars.
- Closer to the Sun, smaller, and made of rock and metal.
- Have solid surfaces, making them terrestrial planets.
- Mercury: smallest, no atmosphere; Venus: hottest, thick atmosphere.
- Earth: only known planet with liquid water and life; Mars: the 'Red Planet'.

<!-- NARRATION: Moving inwards, we encounter the terrestrial, or rocky, planets: Mercury, Venus, Earth, and Mars. These worlds are closer to the Sun and have solid surfaces, each with unique characteristics. -->

## The Outer Planets: Gas and Ice Giants
- Jupiter, Saturn, Uranus, and Neptune.
- Larger, farther from the Sun, composed mainly of gases (hydrogen, helium).
- Also known as Jovian planets.
- Jupiter: largest, Great Red Spot; Saturn: famous rings.
- Uranus: tilted on its side; Neptune: farthest, strong winds.

<!-- NARRATION: Further out lie the gas and ice giants: Jupiter, Saturn, Uranus, and Neptune. These colossal planets are made mostly of gas and are much farther from the Sun, boasting their own incredible features like Saturn's rings. -->

---

## Beyond the Planets: The Kuiper Belt & Oort Cloud
- Regions beyond Neptune containing icy bodies and dwarf planets.
- Kuiper Belt: home to Pluto and many comets.
- Oort Cloud: a theoretical spherical shell of icy objects.
- Source of many long-period comets.
- These regions are remnants from the solar system's formation.
