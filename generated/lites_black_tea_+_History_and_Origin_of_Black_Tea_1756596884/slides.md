## Ancient Roots in China
- Tea originated in China over 5,000 years ago.
- Early tea was likely consumed for medicinal purposes.
- The discovery of oxidation led to different tea types.
- Wuyi Mountains: a cradle of early tea culture.
- The Ming Dynasty marked a shift towards processing.

<!-- NARRATION: The story of tea begins in ancient China, with evidence suggesting its use over five millennia ago. ## The Birth of Black Tea
- Black tea, known as 'hong cha' (red tea) in China.
- Developed from the desire for a more robust, oxidized tea.
- The process of full oxidation gives it its distinct color and flavor.
- This fermentation process was a significant innovation.
- Initially popular for its taste and extended shelf life.

<!-- NARRATION: Black tea, or 'red tea' as it's known in China, emerged as a distinct category through innovation in processing. This fermentation was a crucial step in diversifying tea production and creating a new favorite. -->

## Global Expansion: The Tea Trade
- The Dutch were among the first to import tea to Europe.
- Tea became a luxury commodity in the 17th century.
- The British East India Company played a crucial role.
- Introduced to Britain and became a national drink.
- This sparked the vast global tea trade we know today.

<!-- NARRATION: The popularity of tea quickly spread beyond China, becoming a highly sought-after commodity in Europe during the 17th century. ## From China to the World
- Demand outstripped Chinese supply, leading to new cultivation.
- Robert Fortune smuggled tea plants and knowledge to India.
- Assam and Darjeeling became major tea-producing regions.
- Sri Lanka (Ceylon) and Kenya also became significant producers.
- Black tea's journey transformed global agriculture and trade.
