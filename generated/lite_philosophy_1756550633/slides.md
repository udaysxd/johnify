## Philosophy
- **Core branches**: Metaphysics (reality), Epistemology (knowledge), Ethics (morality), Logic (reasoning), Aesthetics (beauty)
- **Socratic Method**: Questioning assumptions to expose contradictions and reach truth (e.g., "What is justice?")
- **Deductive reasoning**: If all humans are mortal (premise 1) and Socrates is human (premise 2), then Socrates is mortal (conclusion)
- **Ethical frameworks**: Utilitarianism (maximize happiness for the greatest number), Deontology (follow moral rules/duties), Virtue Ethics (cultivate character)
- **Famous thought experiment**: Descartes' "I think, therefore I am" establishes certainty of one's own existence through doubt

## Core Ideas
- **Socratic Method**: Questioning assumptions to expose contradictions and seek truth through dialogue (e.g., "What is justice?").
- **Mind-Body Dualism**: Descartes' argument that the mind (non-physical) and body (physical) are distinct substances ("I think, therefore I am").
- **Utilitarianism**: Bentham and Mill's ethical theory: actions are right if they maximize happiness for the greatest number of people.
- **Categorical Imperative**: Kant's moral rule: act only according to principles that could become universal laws.
- **Existentialism**: Emphasis on individual freedom, choice, and responsibility in creating meaning (e.g., Sartre: "existence precedes essence").

## Examples
- **Socratic Method**: A dialectical approach using questioning to expose contradictions and stimulate critical thinking, e.g., "What is justice?" in Plato's *Republic*.
- **Categorical Imperative**: Kant’s moral rule: "Act only according to that maxim whereby you can at the same time will that it should become a universal law."
- **Cogito, ergo sum**: Descartes' foundational claim ("I think, therefore I am") establishing certainty from doubt in *Meditations*.
- **Utilitarianism**: Bentham and Mill's principle: actions are right if they promote the greatest happiness for the greatest number.
- **Allegory of the Cave**: Plato’s metaphor for enlightenment, depicting prisoners mistaking shadows for reality until one escapes.

## Summary
- Philosophy examines four core branches: metaphysics (reality), epistemology (knowledge), ethics (morality), and logic (reasoning).
- Socratic questioning uses a 5-step method to challenge assumptions and clarify concepts through disciplined dialogue.
- Utilitarianism, a major ethical theory, argues an action is moral if it produces the greatest good for the greatest number.
- The mind-body problem debates whether consciousness is purely physical (materialism) or non-physical (dualism).
- A key critical thinking tip: always identify the underlying premises of an argument before evaluating its conclusion.
