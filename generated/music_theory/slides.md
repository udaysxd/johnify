## What is the name of the scale that consists of five notes per octave: Core Concept

![What is the name of the scale that consists of five notes per octave Concept](https://picsum.photos/seed/b3ac8b4ac095dba83eb30110eb966a6b/800/300)

The Pentatonic scale consists of five notes per octave, which is why it's called 'pentatonic' \(penta meaning five\)\. The Chromatic scale includes all twelve notes, the Diatonic scale has seven notes, and the Whole tone scale consists of six notes each a whole step apart\.

*Key points to remember:*
- The Pentatonic scale consists of five notes per octave, which is why it's called 'pentatonic' \(penta meaning five\)\.
- The Chromatic scale includes all twelve notes, the Diatonic scale has seven notes, and the Whole tone scale consists of six notes each a whole step apart\.
- This concept is fundamental to understanding the topic.

## Which interval is known as 'The Devil's Interval' due to its dissonance: Core Concept

![Which interval is known as 'The Devil's Interval' due to its dissonance Concept](https://picsum.photos/seed/21fb07d9c9193276725c3a425df4fa4c/800/300)

The Tritone, an interval of three whole tones \(hence the name\), is known as 'The Devil's Interval' because of its highly dissonant sound\. The Perfect fifth and Octave are consonant intervals, and the Major third, while slightly less consonant, is not considered dissonant\.

*Key points to remember:*
- The Tritone, an interval of three whole tones \(hence the name\), is known as 'The Devil's Interval' because of its highly dissonant sound\.
- The Perfect fifth and Octave are consonant intervals, and the Major third, while slightly less consonant, is not considered dissonant\.
- This concept is fundamental to understanding the topic.

## In music theory, what does 'forte' indicate: Core Concept

![In music theory, what does 'forte' indicate Concept](https://picsum.photos/seed/bcfacc343a2eb56462ad961d6553267a/800/300)

'Forte' is a dynamic marking in music that instructs the musician to play loudly\. 'Piano' \(not to be confused with the instrument\) means to play softly, while terms like 'allegro' and 'lento' indicate speed \(quickly and slowly, respectively\)\.

*Key points to remember:*
- 'Forte' is a dynamic marking in music that instructs the musician to play loudly\.
- 'Piano' \(not to be confused with the instrument\) means to play softly, while terms like 'allegro' and 'lento' indicate speed \(quickly and slowly, respectively\)\.
- This concept is fundamental to understanding the topic.
