## The Art of Chicken Fried Rice
- **Introduction**: A classic, versatile dish with deep roots in Asian cuisine
- **Key characteristics**: Wok hei (breath of the wok), separate grains, balanced flavors
- **Core components**: Rice, chicken, vegetables, eggs, and seasonings

## Ingredient Preparation
- **Rice preparation**: Cook 1 day ahead, refrigerate uncovered to dry
- **Chicken marinade**: 15 minutes in soy sauce, cornstarch, and rice wine
- **Vegetable prep**: Uniform dicing (¼-inch) for quick, even cooking
- **Mise en place**: All ingredients measured and within reach before heating

<!-- NARRATION: Proper preparation is crucial for perfect fried rice. Start by cooking your rice a day ahead and refrigerating it uncovered to dry out. Marinate diced chicken for 15 minutes in soy sauce, cornstarch, and rice wine. Dice all vegetables uniformly to about a quarter-inch for quick, even cooking. Finally, practice mise en place—have everything measured and within reach before you start heating the wok. This sets you up for success! -->

## Sauce Integration & Seasoning
- **Sauce mixture**: 2 tbsp light soy, 1 tbsp dark soy, 1 tbsp oyster sauce, 1 tsp sugar
- **Application**: Pour around edges of wok (sizzles and caramelizes)
- **Tossing**: Immediate vigorous tossing to coat every grain
- **Seasoning balance**: Taste and adjust with white pepper, salt if needed

<!-- NARRATION: Now, let's talk sauce and seasoning. Mix together two tablespoons of light soy sauce, one tablespoon each of dark soy and oyster sauce, and a teaspoon of sugar. When it's time to add it, pour the sauce around the edges of the hot wok so it sizzles and caramelizes. Immediately toss everything vigorously to coat every grain of rice. Taste and adjust with white pepper or salt if needed for that perfect balance. -->

## Common Mistakes to Avoid
- **Using fresh rice**: Results in mushy, sticky fried rice
- **Overcrowding wok**: Lowers temperature, causes steaming instead of frying
- **Over-saucing**: Makes rice soggy and overly salty
- **Insufficient heat**: Fails to create characteristic smoky flavor

<!-- NARRATION: Avoid these common mistakes to ensure your fried rice turns out great. Never use fresh rice—it will make your dish mushy and sticky. Don't overcrowd the wok, as it lowers the temperature and causes steaming instead of frying. Be careful not to over-sauce, which can make the rice soggy and too salty. And always use the highest heat possible to achieve that characteristic smoky flavor and proper texture. -->

## Plating & Serving Suggestions
- **Traditional**: Serve in warmed bowl or plate immediately
- **Garnishes**: Additional scallions, cilantro, or fried shallots
- **Pairings**: Egg drop soup, steamed vegetables, or pickled side dishes
- **Leftover tip**: Re-fry briefly to restore texture (never microwave)

<!-- NARRATION: Time to plate and serve! Traditionally, fried rice is served immediately in a warmed bowl or plate. Consider garnishing with extra scallions, cilantro, or fried shallots for added flavor and appeal. It pairs wonderfully with egg drop soup, steamed vegetables, or pickled side dishes. If you have leftovers, remember to briefly re-fry them to restore texture—never microwave, as it will make the rice soft and lose its delightful crispness. -->

## Recipe Quick Reference
- **Yield**: 4 servings
- **Active time**: 25 minutes
- **Key ratios**: 3 cups rice : 250g chicken : 2 eggs : 1 cup mixed vegetables
- **Heat level**: Highest possible throughout cooking process
- **Success signs**: Separate grains, slight char, balanced savory flavor
