## Introduction to brazil samba
- **Brazilian samba** is a vibrant, rhythmic dance and music genre originating from Afro-Brazilian communities, characterized by **percussive beats**, flowing movements, and colorful costumes.
- Samba is a **cultural symbol of Brazil**, deeply tied to Carnival and representing joy, resilience, and the country's diverse heritage.
- An estimated **2 million people** dance samba daily in Rio de Janeiro alone, making it one of the most practiced dances in the world.

## Key Concepts
- **Samba's Afro-Brazilian roots** - Samba originated from African rhythms brought by enslaved people, blending with Indigenous and Portuguese influences. It matters because it reflects Brazil's **cultural diversity** and history of resistance.
- **The role of percussion** - Samba is driven by instruments like the **surdo** (bass drum) and **tamborim** (small drum), creating its signature energetic beat. This matters because the rhythm is the heartbeat of samba, uniting dancers and musicians.
- **Carnival and samba schools** - Samba is central to **Rio's Carnival**, where schools compete with elaborate parades and costumes. This matters because it showcases community pride, creativity, and keeps the tradition alive.
