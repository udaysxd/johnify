## Introduction to anti ageing
- Anti-aging refers to the **scientific and lifestyle approaches** that aim to slow, prevent, or reverse the biological processes of aging to maintain youthfulness and vitality for as long as possible.
- It is critically important because it focuses on **extending healthspan**, the period of life spent in good health, which improves quality of life and reduces the burden of age-related diseases on individuals and healthcare systems.
- An interesting statistic is that by 2030, **1 in 6 people** in the world will be aged 60 years or over, making research into healthy aging more relevant than ever for global populations.

## Key Concepts
- **Cellular senescence** refers to when cells stop dividing but don't die, accumulating over time and releasing inflammatory factors that damage nearby tissues; this matters because clearing these senescent cells has been shown to delay age-related diseases and extend healthspan in animal models.
- **Telomere shortening** occurs as protective caps on chromosome ends gradually erode with each cell division, eventually triggering cellular aging or death; this process is significant because maintaining telomere length through lifestyle factors and potential therapies may slow cellular aging and reduce age-related degeneration.
- **Oxidative stress** describes the imbalance between free radicals and antioxidants in the body, causing cumulative damage to proteins, lipids, and DNA; this is crucial because reducing oxidative damage through antioxidant-rich diets and lifestyle interventions can slow aging processes and lower disease risk.

## How anti ageing Works
- **Protection from damage** involves shielding skin from UV rays and environmental stressors to prevent collagen breakdown and DNA mutations, which is crucial because it stops up to 80% of visible aging before it starts.
- **Cellular repair and renewal** uses ingredients like retinoids and peptides to stimulate collagen production and accelerate skin cell turnover, which is vital because it reverses existing damage and restores skin's youthful thickness and elasticity.
- **Moisture and barrier support** focuses on hydrating ingredients like hyaluronic acid and ceramides to strengthen the skin's protective layer and prevent moisture loss, which is essential because well-hydrated skin appears plumper, smoother, and more resilient to wrinkles.

## Examples of anti ageing
- **Retinoids (like tretinoin)** are vitamin A derivatives that increase skin cell turnover and collagen production. They are clinically proven to reduce fine lines, improve skin texture, and fade sun-induced hyperpigmentation.
- **Hyaluronic Acid Fillers** are injectable gels that restore lost volume by binding water in the skin. They are highly relevant for smoothing deep wrinkles, plumping lips, and recontouring areas like the cheeks that lose fullness with age.
- **Antioxidants (Vitamins C & E)** are topical serums that neutralize free radicals from UV exposure and pollution. Their daily use is crucial for preventing premature photoaging by protecting the skin's collagen and reducing oxidative damage.

## Summary: anti ageing
- **Cellular senescence** is a key driver of aging where cells stop dividing but remain metabolically active, producing inflammatory signals that damage surrounding tissues. This matters because targeting senescent cells through **senolytics** represents one of the most promising therapeutic approaches to delay multiple age-related diseases and extend healthspan.
- **Telomere shortening** occurs naturally with each cell division and serves as a biological clock for cellular aging. This is significant because maintaining telomere length through lifestyle factors and potential therapies may slow cellular aging processes, though interventions require careful regulation to avoid cancer risks from excessive cell proliferation.
- **Epigenetic changes** accumulate throughout life and alter gene expression patterns without changing the DNA sequence itself. These changes matter because they serve as reversible markers of biological aging, with emerging **epigenetic clocks** providing precise aging measurements and potential targets for interventions to reset cellular age.
