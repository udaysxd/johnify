## Newton Scientist
- Formulated three laws of motion: inertia (F=0 ⇒ a=0), F=ma, and action-reaction (1687)
- Developed universal law of gravitation: F = G(m₁m₂)/r² with G ≈ 6.674×10⁻¹¹ N·m²/kg²
- Invented calculus (fluxions) simultaneously with Leibniz, documented in *Method of Fluxions* (1671)
- Constructed the first practical reflecting telescope using a spherical mirror in 1668
- Published *Philosophiæ Naturalis Principia Mathematica* in 1687, revolutionizing physics
- Demonstrated white light composition through prism experiments, identifying 7 spectral colors

## Core Ideas
- **Three Laws of Motion**: 1) An object at rest stays at rest, and an object in motion stays in motion unless acted upon by an external force (inertia). 2) Force equals mass times acceleration (F=ma). 3) For every action, there is an equal and opposite reaction.
- **Universal Law of Gravitation**: Every particle attracts every other particle with a force proportional to the product of their masses and inversely proportional to the square of the distance between them (F = G(m₁m₂)/r²).
- **Calculus Development**: Independently invented calculus (fluxions) to solve problems of motion and changing quantities, providing a mathematical framework for his physical laws.
- **Experimental Method**: Emphasized hypothesis testing through controlled experiments, such as using prisms to demonstrate that white light is composed of a spectrum of colors.
- **Laws of Cooling**: Formulated that the rate of heat loss of a body is proportional to the temperature difference between the body and its surroundings.

## How To Learn
- **Engage in systematic experimentation**: Newton conducted over 1,000 experiments with prisms to derive his theory of color, demonstrating the value of repeated, methodical testing.
- **Master foundational mathematics first**: Newton invented calculus to solve problems in physics; ensure you have strong algebra and geometry skills before tackling advanced topics.
- **Document observations meticulously**: Newton’s notebooks contained detailed records of every experiment and calculation—maintain a dedicated journal for hypotheses, data, and reflections.
- **Study interdisciplinary connections**: Newton linked physics, mathematics, and astronomy; explore how concepts like gravity apply across fields to deepen understanding.
- **Challenge existing paradigms critically**: Newton questioned Aristotle’s theories by testing inertia and motion; always probe assumptions with evidence rather than accepting dogma.
- **Apply problems to real-world contexts**: Newton used falling apples and planetary orbits to model gravity; relate abstract principles to tangible phenomena to reinforce learning.

## Examples
- **Law of Universal Gravitation**: Every particle attracts every other particle with a force proportional to the product of their masses and inversely proportional to the square of the distance between them (F = G(m₁m₂)/r²)
- **Three Laws of Motion**: 1) Objects remain at rest or in uniform motion unless acted upon; 2) F=ma (force equals mass times acceleration); 3) Every action has an equal and opposite reaction
- **Calculus Development**: Independently invented differential and integral calculus to solve problems in physics and geometry
- **Optics Experiments**: Used prisms to demonstrate white light composition, showing refraction angles differ by color (e.g., red light bends least)
- **Mathematical Principles**: Published "Philosophiæ Naturalis Principia Mathematica" (1687) containing derivations of planetary motions and gravitational theory
- **Reflecting Telescope**: Built the first practical reflecting telescope using a curved mirror instead of lenses to avoid chromatic aberration

## Summary
- Formulated the three laws of motion, including the second law expressed as F = ma, quantifying force in newtons (N).
- Developed the law of universal gravitation, F = G(m₁m₂)/r², with G ≈ 6.674×10⁻¹¹ N·m²/kg².
- Invented calculus independently of Leibniz to solve problems involving changing quantities and motion.
- Constructed the first practical reflecting telescope in 1668, using a mirror to avoid chromatic aberration.
- Published "Philosophiæ Naturalis Principia Mathematica" in 1687, laying the foundation for classical mechanics.
