## Introduction to shark tank
- **Shark Tank** is a reality television show where entrepreneurs pitch their businesses to a panel of wealthy investors, called "sharks," who decide whether to invest their own money
- It's culturally relevant because it **democratizes entrepreneurship** by showcasing how business deals work and inspiring innovation across diverse industries
- Since its premiere, sharks have invested over **$200 million** in more than 400 businesses, creating an economic impact worth billions

## Key Concepts
- **Valuation** determines what percentage of the company an entrepreneur trades for an investment, and it matters because it directly sets the ownership stake and financial relationship between the founders and the Sharks.
- **Equity** is the ownership share an entrepreneur offers to an investor in exchange for capital, and it matters because giving up too much can mean losing control of the company's future decisions.
- The **Ask** is the specific amount of money an entrepreneur requests and what they are willing to give up for it, and it matters because it sets the entire tone for the negotiation and reveals the founder's preparation and understanding of their business's worth.

## How shark tank Works
- **Entrepreneurs pitch their business**: Founders present their product or service to the Sharks, explaining their company's value and financials, which establishes the foundation for potential investment negotiations.
- **Sharks ask probing questions**: Investors scrutinize the business model, market potential, and financial health to assess risks and opportunities, ensuring they make informed decisions before offering any deals.
- **Negotiation and deal-making**: Sharks make offers, often requesting equity or royalties in exchange for capital and expertise, which can transform the business if both parties agree to terms.

## Examples of shark tank
- **Scrub Daddy**: A smiley-faced scrubber that changes texture based on water temperature, becoming firm in cold water and soft in warm water. This product demonstrates how **patent protection** and a unique material innovation can create a defensible market position, becoming the most successful Shark Tank product with over $200 million in sales.
- **Squatty Potty**: A simple bathroom stool designed to improve bodily alignment during bathroom use, accompanied by memorable marketing campaigns. This illustrates how **disrupting everyday products** with functional improvements and viral marketing can turn a taboo subject into a massive commercial success, generating over $200 million in revenue.
