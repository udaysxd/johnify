## Marie Curie
- Born Maria Skłodowska in Warsaw, Poland, on November 7, 1867; moved to Paris in 1891 to study at the Sorbonne.
- Discovered two radioactive elements: polonium (named after her native Poland) in 1898 and radium later that same year.
- First woman to win a Nobel Prize (1903, Physics, shared with Pierre Curie and Henri Becquerel) and first person to win a second Nobel Prize (1911, Chemistry).
- Died on July 4, 1934, from aplastic anemia, almost certainly caused by prolonged exposure to ionizing radiation from her research.
- Her notebooks and personal papers remain highly radioactive and are stored in lead-lined boxes, requiring special handling.

## Core Ideas
- Discovered two radioactive elements: polonium (1898, named after her homeland) and radium (isolated in 1910)
- Pioneered the theory of radioactivity (a term she coined) and developed techniques for isolating radioactive isotopes
- First woman to win a Nobel Prize (1903, Physics) and first person to win a second Nobel (1911, Chemistry)
- Her notebooks and personal effects remain highly radioactive and are stored in lead-lined boxes
- Established mobile radiography units ("Petites Curies") to provide X-rays for wounded soldiers in WWI
- Died in 1934 from aplastic anemia, directly linked to her prolonged exposure to ionizing radiation

## How To Learn
- **Dedicate 4–6 hours daily to focused study**, as Curie did while preparing for her university entrance exams in Paris, often working late into the night with minimal resources.
- **Combine theoretical knowledge with hands-on experimentation**; for instance, process tons of pitchblende ore (like Curie’s 4 years processing several tons to isolate radium) to grasp practical challenges and validate concepts.
- **Maintain detailed, dated lab notebooks** for every observation and result—Curie’s notebooks, filled with precise measurements and methods, remain radioactive a century later due to their specificity.
- **Collaborate with a peer or mentor** to critique and deepen understanding, mirroring the Curies' partnership where Pierre verified Marie’s theories and co-developed techniques like the electrometer for radiation measurement.
- **Teach concepts to others** to solidify your grasp; Curie lectured at the Sorbonne and mentored students, forcing clarity in explaining complex radioactivity principles.

## Examples
- **First woman to win a Nobel Prize (1903)**, awarded in Physics for her work on radioactivity, shared with Pierre Curie and Henri Becquerel.
- **First person to win two Nobel Prizes**, earning her second in Chemistry (1911) for isolating pure radium and determining its atomic weight.
- **Discovered two radioactive elements**: polonium (named after her homeland Poland) and radium, isolating 0.1 grams of radium chloride from several tons of pitchblende.
- **Pioneered mobile X-ray units** during WWI, directing the installation of 20 radiological cars and 200 fixed units, personally training 150 women to operate them.
- **Founded the Radium Institute** in Paris (now the Curie Institute), which produced four additional Nobel Prize winners, including her daughter Irène Joliot-Curie.

## Summary
- First woman to win a Nobel Prize (1903, Physics) and only person to win in two different sciences (1911, Chemistry)
- Discovered two radioactive elements: polonium (named after her homeland Poland) and radium
- Pioneered the use of mobile radiography units ("petites Curies") during World War I, treating an estimated 1 million soldiers
- Died in 1934 from aplastic anemia, likely caused by prolonged exposure to radiation
- Her notebooks and personal effects remain highly radioactive and are stored in lead-lined boxes
