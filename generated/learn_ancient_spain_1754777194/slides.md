## Introduction to ancient spain
- **Ancient Spain** refers to the period from **prehistoric times** to the **fall of the Roman Empire**, encompassing diverse cultures like the **Iberians, Celts, Phoenicians, and Romans**.
- It’s **historically significant** as a cultural crossroads, where Mediterranean and European civilizations merged, shaping Spain’s later identity.
- **Fun fact:** The **Roman city of Tarraco (modern Tarragona)** had one of the largest amphitheaters in the empire, seating **14,000 spectators**.

## Slide 2
- **Iberian Peninsula's Diverse Cultures** - Ancient Spain was home to **multiple civilizations**, including Iberians, Celts, and Tartessians, each with unique languages and traditions. This diversity shaped Spain's **cultural richness** and influenced later Roman and Moorish rule.
- **Roman Hispania** - The Romans conquered Spain in **218 BCE**, bringing Latin, roads, and cities like Tarraco (modern Tarragona). This laid the foundation for Spain's **legal systems, language, and urban development**.
- **Phoenician and Greek Trade Networks** - Early Mediterranean traders like the **Phoenicians and Greeks** established colonies (e.g., Gadir/Cádiz) for resources like silver. Their networks connected Spain to **global commerce**, boosting its economic importance.

## Examples of ancient spain
- **Tartessos**: An advanced **Bronze Age civilization** in southern Spain (circa 9th–6th centuries BCE), known for its **metalworking** and trade with Phoenicians. Its relevance lies in being one of the first **complex societies** in Iberia, blending local and foreign influences.
- **Numantia**: A **Celtiberian city** famous for its fierce resistance against Rome in the **Numantine War** (153–133 BCE). Its relevance highlights the **struggle for independence** and Rome's eventual dominance in the Iberian Peninsula.
- **Baelo Claudia**: A well-preserved **Roman coastal town** in Andalusia, known for its **fish-salting industry** and trade with North Africa. Its relevance showcases **Roman urbanization** and economic practices in ancient Spain.
