## Introduction to climate change
- Climate change refers to **long-term shifts** in global temperature patterns and weather conditions, primarily driven by human activities like burning fossil fuels and deforestation
- This issue affects every person on Earth through **extreme weather events**, rising sea levels, and threats to food and water security
- The last decade was the **hottest in 125,000 years**, with global temperatures now approximately 1.1°C warmer than pre-industrial levels

## Key Concepts
- **Greenhouse effect**: Earth's atmosphere traps heat from the sun using gases like carbon dioxide, which keeps our planet warm enough for life; this matters because human activities are intensifying this effect, causing global temperatures to rise beyond natural levels.
- **Carbon footprint**: This measures the total greenhouse gases produced by human activities, directly linking individual and collective actions to climate impact; it matters because reducing our carbon footprint is essential to slowing global warming and mitigating severe environmental consequences.
- **Climate feedback loops**: These are processes where a change in the climate triggers further changes, amplifying warming effects—like melting ice reducing reflectivity and absorbing more heat; they matter because they can accelerate climate change unpredictably, making solutions more urgent and complex.

## How climate change Works
- **Greenhouse gas accumulation**: Human activities like burning fossil fuels release gases that trap heat in the atmosphere, creating a thicker insulating layer around Earth. This is important because these gases act like a blanket, preventing heat from escaping into space and causing the planet to warm.
- **Energy imbalance**: More heat gets trapped than can radiate back into space, causing Earth's overall energy system to become unbalanced. This matters because the excess energy accumulates primarily in the oceans and atmosphere, driving changes in global climate patterns and weather systems.
- **System-wide changes**: The extra heat disrupts established climate patterns, leading to more extreme weather, melting ice, and rising sea levels worldwide. This is critical because these changes impact ecosystems, food security, water resources, and human settlements across the globe.

## Examples of climate change
- **Arctic sea ice decline**: The Arctic has lost over 40% of its summer sea ice volume since 1979, with the oldest and thickest ice disappearing fastest. This demonstrates a major **polar amplification** effect where climate change impacts are more severe at the poles, contributing to global sea level rise and altered weather patterns.
- **Coral reef bleaching**: Rising ocean temperatures have caused mass bleaching events, killing vast sections of reefs like the Great Barrier Reef where over 50% of shallow-water corals died in 2016-2017. This shows how climate change directly threatens **marine biodiversity** and the coastal protection and fisheries that millions of people depend on.
- **Increased extreme weather events**: Climate change has intensified hurricanes, droughts, and wildfires, exemplified by events like Hurricane Harvey (2017) which dropped unprecedented rainfall due to warmer ocean waters. This illustrates how a warming climate **amplifies natural disasters**, increasing their frequency, duration, and destructive power.

## Summary: climate change
- Global temperatures have risen approximately 1.1°C since pre-industrial times, primarily due to **human activities** like burning fossil fuels; this warming is accelerating climate disruptions worldwide, making immediate emission reductions critical to prevent irreversible ecological damage.
- Sea levels are rising at their fastest rate in 3,000 years, threatening coastal communities and ecosystems; this is caused by thermal expansion of seawater and melting ice sheets, highlighting the urgent need for adaptation strategies and infrastructure resilience.
- Climate change intensifies extreme weather events, including heatwaves, droughts, and heavy rainfall; these events endanger food security, water supplies, and public health, underscoring why global cooperation and policy action are essential to mitigate risks.
