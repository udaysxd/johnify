## What is Absinthe?
- Green spirit, high alcohol content
- Key ingredient: wormwood (Artemisia absinthium)
- Flavored with anise, fennel, and herbs
- Distinctive 'lucidity' or clouding when water is added

<!-- NARRATION: Absinthe is a high-proof distilled alcoholic beverage, primarily known for its distinctive green hue. Its signature flavor comes from wormwood, along with anise, fennel, and other botanicals, creating a unique sensory experience. -->

---

## Early Origins (18th Century)
- Origins debated: medicinal elixir or alcoholic drink?
- Developed in Switzerland, popularized in France
- Early versions were less potent and herbaceous
- Became a favored drink among French society

<!-- NARRATION: The origins of absinthe trace back to the 18th century, initially as a medicinal tonic in Switzerland before gaining immense popularity as a social drink in France. Early versions were often simpler and less intensely flavored than what we know today. -->

---

## The Golden Age & The Demonization
- Late 19th Century: "The Green Fairy"
- Associated with artists and bohemians: Van Gogh, Toulouse-Lautrec
- Blamed for social ills, madness, and violence
- Taboo due to alleged hallucinogenic properties of thujone

<!-- NARRATION: The late 19th century saw absinthe's peak popularity, earning it the nickname "The Green Fairy" and becoming closely linked with artistic and bohemian culture. However, it was soon demonized, wrongly blamed for madness and societal decay due to perceived hallucinogenic properties. -->

---

## The Ban and Revival
- Banned in France in 1915, then many other countries
- Forced underground or produced illegally
- Modern absinthe production revived in the late 20th century
- Focus on historical recipes and distillation methods

<!-- NARRATION: Due to widespread public fear and lobbying, absinthe was banned in France in 1915 and subsequently in many other nations, leading to a long period of illegality. Only in recent decades has its production and sale been legally revived, adhering to modern safety standards. -->

---

## The Mystique Today
- Still carries an air of mystery and allure
- Modern absinthe is safe and regulated
- Experiencing a resurgence in popularity globally
- Appreciated for its complex flavor and ritualistic preparation

<!-- NARRATION: Today, absinthe's mystique continues, though its reputation has been cleared of past accusations. It's now appreciated for its complex flavors, historical significance, and the ritualistic way it's prepared and enjoyed, making it a fascinating spirit to explore. -->
