## Introduction to ancient austria
- **Ancient Austria** refers to the region of modern-day Austria during **prehistoric and early historic periods**, including the Celtic, Roman, and early medieval eras.
- It’s **historically significant** as a crossroads of cultures, trade, and empires, shaping Central Europe’s development.
- **Interesting fact**: The **Roman city of Carnuntum**, near Vienna, was one of the empire’s largest military bases, housing over 50,000 people at its peak.

## Slide 2
- **The Hallstatt Culture**: This was a **dominant Iron Age society** (800–400 BCE) in ancient Austria, known for its advanced metalworking and salt mining. It matters because it laid the foundation for **trade networks** and cultural exchange across Europe.
- **Roman Rule (Noricum Province)**: Austria became part of the **Roman Empire** as the province of Noricum, known for its iron production and strategic location. This matters because it integrated Austria into **Rome’s economy and defense systems**, leaving lasting infrastructure.
- **Celtic Tribes (e.g., the Taurisci)**: Before Roman conquest, **Celtic tribes** inhabited Austria, contributing to its early cultural identity. This matters because their **art, language, and social structures** influenced later regional developments.

## Examples of ancient austria

- Its relevance lies in giving the **Hallstatt period** its name, a key phase in European prehistoric archaeology.
